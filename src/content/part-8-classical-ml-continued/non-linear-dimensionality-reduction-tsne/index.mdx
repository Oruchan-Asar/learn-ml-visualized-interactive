<Hook>
  PCA only ever draws straight lines through your data — it can't bend to follow a curve. If two points are genuinely close in some high-dimensional, twisty sense but far apart along every straight axis, PCA has no way to notice. What kind of algorithm *can*?
</Hook>

<Intuition>
  <IntuitionDemo />

  Two true pairs, $P_1$/$P_2$ and $P_3$/$P_4$, start jumbled together at random. After training, the pairs pull apart from each other and tighten internally — nothing here promises *where* each pair ends up, only that points that were neighbors stay neighbors.
</Intuition>

<Formalize>
  **t-SNE** converts distances into probabilities in both spaces, then makes the low-dimensional probabilities match the high-dimensional ones as closely as possible.

  $$
  p_{ij} \propto \exp\!\left(-\frac{\lVert x_i - x_j \rVert^2}{2\sigma^2}\right), \qquad q_{ij} \propto \frac{1}{1 + \lVert y_i - y_j \rVert^2}
  $$

  <Variables>
    <Variable>**$p_{ij}$** — how similar points $i,j$ are in the *original* high-dimensional space, from a Gaussian centered on each point.</Variable>
    <Variable>**$q_{ij}$** — how similar their images $y_i, y_j$ are in the *low-dimensional* embedding, from a heavier-tailed Student-t distribution.</Variable>
    <Variable>**$\sigma$** — the Gaussian's bandwidth (fixed here; real t-SNE solves for it per point to hit a target "perplexity").</Variable>
  </Variables>

  <Steps>
    <Step label="A heavier-tailed kernel in low dimensions, on purpose">
      Squeezing high-dimensional neighborhoods into 2D leaves less room for everyone — the Student-t kernel's
      heavy tails let moderately-distant points spread out further in the embedding without being punished,
      easing that crowding.
    </Step>
    <Step label="Minimize how much Q disagrees with P">
      Gradient descent adjusts every embedded point $y_i$ to make $q_{ij}$ track $p_{ij}$ as closely as
      possible — the **KL divergence** between the two distributions is what's actually being minimized.
    </Step>
    <Step label="Only local structure is preserved">
      Nothing in this objective cares about the *absolute* distance between two far-apart points, or which
      direction a cluster lands in — only whether true neighbors stay neighbors survives the compression.
    </Step>
  </Steps>
</Formalize>

<Play>
  <PlayDemo />

  Drag the steps slider. KL divergence doesn't fall smoothly — early on it can briefly get *worse* before the optimizer finds a better arrangement, the same way loss curves elsewhere in this course occasionally tick upward before a bigger drop. Given enough steps, it settles low and stays there.
</Play>

<WorkedExample>
  Two tight pairs, $(0,0)$–$(1,0)$ and $(20,0)$–$(21,0)$, twenty units apart from each other:

  <Steps>
    <Step label="Within-pair affinity">
      With $\sigma=1$, $P_1$'s unnormalized Gaussian weights on $P_2$, $P_3$, $P_4$ are
      $e^{-1^2/2}\approx0.607$, $e^{-20^2/2}\approx0$, $e^{-21^2/2}\approx0$ — the far points contribute
      nothing measurable. Normalizing: $p_{2\mid1} = 0.607/0.607 \approx 1$, and the same reasoning from
      $P_2$'s side gives $p_{1\mid2}\approx1$. Symmetrizing over $n=4$ points:

      $$
      p_{12} = \frac{p_{2\mid1}+p_{1\mid2}}{2n} \approx \frac{1+1}{2\times4} = 0.25
      $$

      and symmetrically $p_{34} \approx 0.25$.
    </Step>
    <Step label="Cross-pair affinity is essentially zero">
      $p_{13}$, between the two far-apart pairs, comes out indistinguishable from $0$ — high-dimensional
      space has already declared these pairs unrelated.
    </Step>
    <Step label="Training pulls each true pair together, blind to the original coordinates">
      Neither $P_1$ nor $P_2$'s *training* process ever sees the numbers $0$, $1$, $20$, or $21$ again once
      $p_{ij}$ is computed — only the affinities. Yet gradient descent still finds an embedding where each
      pair ends up close and the two pairs end up apart, purely by matching $q_{ij}$ to $p_{ij}$.
    </Step>
  </Steps>
</WorkedExample>

<Summary>
  $$
  p_{ij} \propto \exp\!\left(-\frac{\lVert x_i - x_j \rVert^2}{2\sigma^2}\right), \qquad q_{ij} \propto \frac{1}{1 + \lVert y_i - y_j \rVert^2}
  $$

  t-SNE gives up everything PCA guarantees — no straight-line projection, no preserved absolute distances, no consistent orientation between runs — in exchange for one thing PCA can't do at all: following genuinely curved structure by preserving *who's a neighbor of whom*. That tradeoff is exactly why t-SNE plots are for looking, not measuring — the axes and distances between separated clusters carry no meaning, only the clustering itself does.
</Summary>
