<Hook>
  k-means needs to be told how many clusters to look for, and it always draws them as round, roughly equal-sized blobs. What about data that comes in two long, thin chains instead — and includes a point that doesn't belong to any group at all?
</Hook>

<Intuition>
  <IntuitionDemo />

  Two short chains of points, plus one point sitting far off on its own. Drag `eps` — the neighborhood radius — and watch clusters appear, merge, and disappear. Too small, and even points one unit apart look unrelated; too large, and two genuinely separate chains fuse into one.
</Intuition>

<Formalize>
  **DBSCAN** — short for **Density-Based Spatial Clustering of Applications with Noise** — never asks how many clusters to expect. Instead, a point is a **core point** if it has enough neighbors within a radius `eps`:

  $$
  \text{core}(i) = |\{j : \lVert x_i - x_j \rVert \leq \text{eps}\}| \geq \text{minPts}
  $$

  <Variables>
    <Variable>**$\text{core}(i)$** — whether point $i$ qualifies as a core point, based on how many neighbors it has.</Variable>
    <Variable>**$x_i, x_j$** — two points in the dataset being compared.</Variable>
    <Variable>**`eps`** — the neighborhood radius: how close two points must be to count as neighbors.</Variable>
    <Variable>**`minPts`** — the minimum number of neighbors within `eps` needed for a point to be a core point.</Variable>
  </Variables>

  A cluster is everything reachable by hopping from core point to core point, radius by radius — like a chain of falling dominoes, each one only needing to be close enough to knock over the next. Anything never reached this way, from any core point, is **noise** — not assigned to any cluster at all.
</Formalize>

<Play>
  <PlayDemo />

  At `eps=0.5`, even points exactly $1$ unit apart aren't neighbors — every point is isolated, and the whole dataset is noise. At `eps=1.5`, both chains connect internally without merging with each other. At `eps=5.0`, the two chains fuse into a single cluster — genuinely different structures, collapsed together by too generous a radius.
</Play>

<WorkedExample>
  Two chains at $y=0$ and $y=5$, points spaced $1$ unit apart within each chain, plus one point at $(10,10)$:

  <Steps>
    <Step label="Checking a core point">
      Point $(1,0)$ has neighbors $(0,0)$ and $(2,0)$, both at distance exactly $1$ — with `eps=1.5` and
      `minPts=1`, that's enough: it's a core point.
    </Step>
    <Step label="The gap between chains">
      The closest pair *across* the two chains is $(0,0)$ and $(0,5)$, at distance exactly $5$. At
      `eps=1.5`, that's nowhere close enough to connect — the chains stay two separate clusters.
    </Step>
    <Step label="The point with no neighbors at all">
      $(10,10)$'s nearest other point is $9.4$ units away — far beyond any reasonable `eps`. It's never a
      core point and never reachable from one, so DBSCAN correctly leaves it as noise, folded into neither
      cluster.
    </Step>
  </Steps>
</WorkedExample>

<Summary>
  $$
  \text{core}(i) = |\{j : \lVert x_i - x_j \rVert \leq \text{eps}\}| \geq \text{minPts}
  $$

  DBSCAN trades k-means' two conveniences — pick $k$ up front, every point belongs to some cluster — for two things k-means can't do at all: clusters of any shape, and an explicit "doesn't belong anywhere" label for genuine outliers. Neither algorithm is strictly better; k-means assumes round, similarly-sized groups and gets a clean answer fast, while DBSCAN makes no shape assumption at all but needs `eps` tuned by hand, the same way $k$ itself did.
</Summary>
