<Hook>
  The diffusion chapter's denoiser always undid noise the same way, regardless of what it was denoising toward. A real image generator starts from the same random noise every time and still needs to draw something different for "a cat" than for "a dog" — so what changes?
</Hook>

<Intuition>
  <IntuitionDemo />

  The exact same starting noise, run through the exact same reverse-diffusion formula from the earlier chapter — the only thing that changes between runs is which caption's predicted-noise sequence gets used at each step.
</Intuition>

<Formalize>
  Nothing about the reverse step changes:

  $$
  x_{t-1} = \frac{x_t - \sqrt{\beta_t}\,\hat\epsilon(x_t, t, \mathbf{c})}{\sqrt{\alpha_t}}
  $$

  <Variables>
    <Variable>**$x_t$** — the noisy value at diffusion step $t$.</Variable>
    <Variable>**$x_{t-1}$** — the slightly-less-noisy value this reverse step produces.</Variable>
    <Variable>**$t$** — the current diffusion timestep.</Variable>
    <Variable>**$\alpha_t$, $\beta_t$** — the same noise-schedule coefficients from the earlier diffusion chapter, controlling how much signal versus noise remains at step $t$.</Variable>
    <Variable>**$\hat\epsilon(x_t, t, \mathbf{c})$** — the denoiser's predicted noise, now taking the caption embedding $\mathbf{c}$ as an extra input.</Variable>
    <Variable>**$\mathbf{c}$** — the text embedding of the caption being generated toward — the one new ingredient this chapter adds.</Variable>
  </Variables>

  <Steps>
    <Step label="One new input: the caption embedding">
      The only addition is $\mathbf{c}$ — a text embedding fed into the denoiser alongside $x_t$ and $t$. Nothing
      else about the reverse step's math changes.
    </Step>
    <Step label="Same state, different prediction, different image">
      A denoiser trained on captioned images learns to predict *different* noise, at the *same* $(x_t, t)$,
      depending on $\mathbf{c}$ — which means the same starting noise unrolls into a different final image for
      every different caption.
    </Step>
  </Steps>
</Formalize>

<Play>
  <PlayDemo />

  One shared starting point, three trajectories. The unconditional path (no caption at all) takes a middle course; "a cat" and "a dog" each pull the same starting noise toward a different final value, in opposite directions.
</Play>

<WorkedExample>
  Shared starting noise $x_T = 2.0$, same noise schedule as the earlier chapter, three different predicted-noise sequences:

  <Steps>
    <Step label="No conditioning: a generic middle-ground result">
      Predicting zero noise at every step reduces the reverse formula to just $x_{t-1}=x_t/\sqrt{\alpha_t}$.
      Unrolling from $x_4=2.0$ with $\alpha=[0.9,0.8,0.7,0.6]$, from $t=3$ down to $t=0$:
      1. $x_3 = 2.0/\sqrt{0.6} \approx 2.582$
      2. $x_2 = 2.582/\sqrt{0.7} \approx 3.086$
      3. $x_1 = 3.086/\sqrt{0.8} \approx 3.450$
      4. $x_0 = 3.450/\sqrt{0.9} \approx 3.637$

      A plain, unconditioned denoising.
    </Step>
    <Step label="'A cat': the same starting noise, steered higher">
      Swapping in the cat-conditioned noise sequence $[-1.2, 0.8, -0.5, 1]$ (read at each step from
      $t{=}3$ down to $t{=}0$) — otherwise identical math. Just the first step already diverges from the
      unconditional case: $x_3 = (2.0-\sqrt{0.4}\times(-1.2))/\sqrt{0.6} \approx (2.0+0.759)/0.775
      \approx3.562$, compared to $2.582$ with no conditioning. Carrying the same three remaining steps
      forward reverses the *same* $x_4=2.0$ to $\approx4.330$ instead.
    </Step>
    <Step label="'A dog': the same starting noise, steered lower">
      The dog-conditioned sequence is $[0.7, -0.3, 0.9, -0.6]$. Its first step:
      $x_3 = (2.0-\sqrt{0.4}\times0.7)/\sqrt{0.6} \approx (2.0-0.443)/0.775 \approx2.010$ — barely moved
      from $2.0$, unlike the cat case's big jump. Carrying the same three remaining steps forward pulls
      that same $x_4=2.0$ down to $\approx2.789$ — on the opposite side of the unconditional baseline
      from the cat result.
    </Step>
  </Steps>

  Same schedule, same formula, same starting noise. Three different captions, three different destinations.
</WorkedExample>

<DiffusionCheckpoint />

<Summary>
  $$
  \hat\epsilon(x_t, t, \mathbf{c}) \quad\text{— add one argument to the denoiser, and the entire generation becomes steerable}
  $$

  Text-conditioned diffusion isn't a different algorithm from plain diffusion — it's the identical reverse process with one extra input threaded through the noise predictor. That single addition is what turns a denoiser into a generator you can actually direct: same random seed, same math, a different picture for every different prompt. The next chapter turns to a modality with its own extra structure that neither images nor audio have on their own: time unfolding across a whole sequence of frames.
</Summary>
