<Hook>
  Audio in, an image out — with nothing hand-built in between except two mechanisms this part already built separately. Retrieval finds the right words; generation turns those words into a picture. Chain them, and you have a tiny multimodal assistant.
</Hook>

<Intuition>
  <IntuitionDemo />

  Pick an audio clip. It retrieves its nearest caption in the shared embedding space — exactly Chapter 1's mechanism — and that caption's identity selects which noise-conditioning sequence steers the reverse-diffusion process — exactly Chapter 2's mechanism.
</Intuition>

<Formalize>
  Two stages, two chapters, one pipeline:

  $$
  \text{audio} \xrightarrow{\text{nearest-neighbor in shared space}} \text{caption} \xrightarrow{\text{selects}} \hat\epsilon(x_t, t, \mathbf{c}) \xrightarrow{\text{reverse diffusion}} \text{generated value}
  $$

  <Variables>
    <Variable>**$\mathbf{c}$** — the text embedding of whichever caption audio retrieval selected, threaded into the denoiser exactly as in the diffusion chapter.</Variable>
    <Variable>**$\hat\epsilon(x_t, t, \mathbf{c})$** — the same conditioned noise predictor from the diffusion chapter, now driven by the retrieved caption's embedding.</Variable>
    <Variable>**$x_t$, $t$** — the noisy diffusion state and current timestep, unchanged from that chapter's process.</Variable>
  </Variables>

  <Steps>
    <Step label="No new mechanism — just reused pieces">
      Nothing here is a new mechanism — it's the exact retrieval rule from audio embeddings, feeding the exact
      conditioning slot from text-conditioned diffusion.
    </Step>
    <Step label="This is the general shape of multimodal systems">
      A working multimodal system is very often just two or three single-modality mechanisms, connected so one's
      output becomes the next one's input.
    </Step>
  </Steps>
</Formalize>

<WorkedExample>
  Three audio inputs, run through the full pipeline:

  <Steps>
    <Step label="Dog barking routes to the dog caption and dog conditioning">
      Retrieval: "Audio: a dog barking" $(1.9,3.2)$ to "Caption: a dog running" $(2.2,2.8)$ —
      $\sqrt{(1.9-2.2)^2+(3.2-2.8)^2}=\sqrt{0.09+0.16}=\sqrt{0.25}=0.5$. Generation: the dog-conditioned
      noise sequence $[0.7,-0.3,0.9,-0.6]$ reverses the shared $x_4=2.0$ (same mechanism as Chapter 2's
      first-step check, $x_3\approx2.010$) all the way to a final value $\approx2.789$.
    </Step>
    <Step label="Cat meowing routes to the cat caption and cat conditioning">
      Retrieval: "Audio: a cat meowing" $(0.1,1.2)$ to "Caption: a cat sleeping" $(0.3,0.8)$ —
      $\sqrt{(0.1-0.3)^2+(1.2-0.8)^2}=\sqrt{0.04+0.16}=\sqrt{0.2}\approx0.45$. Generation: the
      cat-conditioned noise sequence $[-1.2,0.8,-0.5,1]$ reverses the same $x_4=2.0$
      (first step $x_3\approx3.562$, per Chapter 2) to $\approx4.330$ — a different destination from the
      same starting point.
    </Step>
    <Step label="Bird chirping completes the set">
      Retrieval: "Audio: a bird chirping" $(4.2,0.2)$ to "Caption: a bird flying" $(3.7,0.4)$ —
      $\sqrt{(4.2-3.7)^2+(0.2-0.4)^2}=\sqrt{0.25+0.04}=\sqrt{0.29}\approx0.54$. Generation: the
      bird-conditioned noise sequence $[-0.2,0.4,-0.9,0.5]$ (read $t{=}3$ down to $t{=}0$), first step
      $x_3=(2.0-\sqrt{0.4}\times(-0.2))/\sqrt{0.6}\approx2.746$, reverses all the way to $\approx3.866$ —
      a third distinct value, confirming all three retrieval-then-generation chains work independently
      and correctly.
    </Step>
  </Steps>
</WorkedExample>

<Summary>

  \text{audio} \to \text{retrieval} \to \text{caption} \to \text{conditioning} \to \text{generation}
  $$

  This is the actual shape of most real multimodal systems: not one enormous end-to-end model, but a handful of well-understood single-modality mechanisms — an embedding space here, a conditioned generator there — wired together so information flows from one to the next.
