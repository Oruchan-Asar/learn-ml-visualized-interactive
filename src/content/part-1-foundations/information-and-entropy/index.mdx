<Hook>
  Which is more surprising: a coin that's fair, or one that always lands heads?
</Hook>

<Intuition>
  <IntuitionDemo />

  Drag the bars from equal toward one outcome dominating. Once an outcome becomes almost certain, there's really nothing left to learn from watching the next draw — you already knew what was coming.
</Intuition>

<Formalize>
  That "amount left to learn" is **entropy**, measured in bits —

  $$
  H(X) = -\sum_i P(x_i) \log_2 P(x_i)
  $$

  <Variables>
    <Variable>**$H(X)$** — the entropy of the distribution, in bits.</Variable>
    <Variable>**$P(x_i)$** — the probability of outcome $x_i$.</Variable>
    <Variable>**$x_i$** — one specific outcome, indexed by $i$.</Variable>
  </Variables>

  It's exactly zero when one outcome is certain, and it's largest exactly when every outcome is equally likely — uncertainty is maximized when you have the least information to favor any one outcome.
</Formalize>

<Play>
  <PlayDemo />

  Drag toward uniform and watch entropy climb to its ceiling; drag toward certainty and watch it collapse toward zero.
</Play>

<WorkedExample>
  With $P = (0.5, 0.25, 0.125, 0.125)$:

  <Steps>
    <Step label="Compute each term, -p log₂ p">
      $$
      0.5(1) = 0.5, \quad 0.25(2) = 0.5, \quad 0.125(3) = 0.375, \quad 0.125(3) = 0.375
      $$

      Here the probabilities are all powers of two, so every term comes out to a whole number of bits.
    </Step>
    <Step label="Add them up for the entropy">
      $$
      H(X) = 0.5+0.5+0.375+0.375 = 1.75 \text{ bits}
      $$
    </Step>
  </Steps>
</WorkedExample>

  $$

</Summary>
