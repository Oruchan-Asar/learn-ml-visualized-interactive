import { Hook, Intuition, Formalize, Play, WorkedExample, Steps, Step, Variables, Variable, Summary } from "@/components/chapter/Beats";
import { IntuitionDemo, PlayDemo, DescentCheckpoint } from "./Demos";

<Hook>
  You know the slope at a point. How do you actually use it to walk downhill to the minimum — and what goes wrong if your steps are too big?
</Hook>

<Intuition>
  <IntuitionDemo />

  Each click takes one step: move a little in the direction that decreases $f$, based on the slope right where you're standing. Watch the trail of dots — that's the path down.
</Intuition>

<Formalize>
  That's **gradient descent**. Starting from some $x_0$, repeat:

  $$
  x_{n+1} = x_n - \eta \, \nabla f(x_n)
  $$

  <Variables>
    <Variable>**$x_n$** — the current position, before this step.</Variable>
    <Variable>**$x_{n+1}$** — the new position, after taking the step.</Variable>
    <Variable>**$\eta$** — the learning rate: how big a step to take (read "eta").</Variable>
    <Variable>**$\nabla f(x_n)$** — the gradient evaluated at the current position, pointing uphill.</Variable>
  </Variables>

  <Steps>
    <Step label="Eta is the learning rate">
      $\eta$ (eta) is the **learning rate** — how big a step you take.
    </Step>
    <Step label="Direction versus distance">
      The gradient tells you *which way* is uphill; $\eta$ decides *how far* you step away from it.
    </Step>
  </Steps>
</Formalize>

<Play>
  <PlayDemo />

  Drag $\eta$ up and take a few steps. Too small and it crawls; push it high enough and it overshoots the minimum, bouncing back and forth — or worse, flies further away with every step.
</Play>

<WorkedExample>
  From $x_0 = 5$ with $\eta = 0.1$:

  <Steps>
    <Step label="Take the first step">
      $$
      x_1 = 5 - 0.1(2 \cdot 5 - 4) = 5 - 0.6 = 4.4
      $$
    </Step>
    <Step label="Take the second step">
      $$
      x_2 = 4.4 - 0.1(2 \cdot 4.4 - 4) = 4.4 - 0.48 = 3.92
      $$
    </Step>
    <Step label="Read what happened">
      Each step shrinks the distance to the minimum at $x^* = 2$ by the same factor — that's what a *stable*
      learning rate looks like.
    </Step>
  </Steps>
</WorkedExample>

<DescentCheckpoint />

<Summary>
  $$
  x_{n+1} = x_n - \eta \, \nabla f(x_n)
  $$

  Gradient descent repeatedly steps against the gradient. The learning rate $\eta$ trades off speed against stability: too small converges slowly, too large overshoots or diverges entirely.
</Summary>
