import { Hook, Intuition, Formalize, Play, WorkedExample, Summary } from "@/components/chapter/Beats";
import { IntuitionDemo, PlayDemo, ChainRuleCheckpoint } from "./Demos";

<Hook>
  If $f$ speeds up as $x$ grows, and $g$ speeds up as $f$ grows, how fast does $g(f(x))$ actually grow?
</Hook>

<Intuition>
  <IntuitionDemo />

  Drag the point on the top curve. Whatever value it lands on, $u$, feeds straight into the bottom curve — the point down there is never dragged directly, it's just wherever $f$ sent it.
</Intuition>

<Formalize>
  Call the whole composition $h(x) = g(f(x))$. Its derivative is the **chain rule** —

  $$
  \frac{dh}{dx} = \frac{dg}{du} \cdot \frac{du}{dx}
  $$

  In words: the combined rate of change is the outer function's slope (at the *inner* value $u$) multiplied by the inner function's slope. Two local slopes, multiplied, give the slope of the whole chain.
</Formalize>

<Play>
  <PlayDemo />

  Drag the top point and watch both slopes update — then check that their product really does track $dh/dx$ as you move.
</Play>

<WorkedExample>
  Here $f(x) = 2x + 1$ and $g(u) = u^2$, so $h(x) = (2x+1)^2$. At $x=1$: $u = f(1) = 3$, so $g'(u) = 2u = 6$ and $f'(x) = 2$. Chain rule: $dh/dx = 6 \times 2 = 12$. Check directly — $h(x) = 4x^2+4x+1$, so $h'(x)=8x+4$, and $h'(1) = 12$. Same answer, two routes.
</WorkedExample>

<ChainRuleCheckpoint />

<Summary>
  $$
  \frac{dh}{dx} = \frac{dg}{du} \cdot \frac{du}{dx}
  $$

  To differentiate a composition, multiply the slopes of each stage. This is the entire mechanism behind backpropagation: a network is just a long chain of compositions, and training multiplies slopes all the way back through it.
</Summary>
