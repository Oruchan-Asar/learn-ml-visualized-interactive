import { Hook, Intuition, Formalize, Play, WorkedExample, Summary } from "@/components/chapter/Beats";
import { IntuitionDemo, PlayDemo, DotProductCheckpoint } from "./Demos";

<Hook>
  How do you measure whether two directions roughly agree, roughly oppose, or have nothing to do with each other?
</Hook>

<Intuition>
  <IntuitionDemo />

  A vector is just an arrow from the origin. Drag $b$ around $a$ and watch the angle $\theta$ between them — that angle alone already tells you "agree," "oppose," or "unrelated."
</Intuition>

<Formalize>
  The **dot product** turns that agreement into a single number —

  $$
  a \cdot b = a_x b_x + a_y b_y
  $$

  which is exactly equal to $|a|\,|b|\cos\theta$. Its sign tells the story: positive when $\theta < 90°$ (roughly the same direction), zero at exactly $90°$ (perpendicular), negative beyond that (roughly opposite).
</Formalize>

<Play>
  <PlayDemo />

  Drag $b$ and watch $a\cdot b$ and $\theta$ move together — the dot product crosses zero at exactly the moment the arrows form a right angle.
</Play>

<WorkedExample>
  With $a=(4,0)$ and $b=(3,4)$: $a\cdot b = 4(3) + 0(4) = 12$. Their magnitudes are $|a|=4$ and $|b|=5$, so $\cos\theta = 12/20 = 0.6$, giving $\theta \approx 53.13°$ — comfortably under $90°$, matching a positive dot product.
</WorkedExample>

<Summary>
  $$
  a \cdot b = a_x b_x + a_y b_y = |a|\,|b|\cos\theta
  $$

  The dot product measures alignment: positive means pointing roughly together, zero means perpendicular, negative means pointing roughly apart.
</Summary>
