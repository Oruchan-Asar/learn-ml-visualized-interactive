import { Hook, Intuition, Formalize, Play, WorkedExample, Steps, Step, Variables, Variable, Summary } from "@/components/chapter/Beats";
import { IntuitionDemo, PlayDemo, DerivativeCheckpoint } from "./Demos";

<Hook>
  You're standing on a curve and can only feel the ground right under your feet — no view of the whole shape. Which way is downhill?
</Hook>

<Intuition>
  <IntuitionDemo />

  Drag the point along the curve. The dashed line is its tangent — tilt down to the right means the curve is falling there; tilt up means it's rising.
</Intuition>

<Formalize>
  That tilt has a name: the **derivative**. It's the instantaneous rate of change of $f$ at the point $x$ —

  $$
  \frac{df}{dx}
  $$

  <Variables>
    <Variable>**$df/dx$** — the derivative of $f$ at the point $x$: how fast $f$ changes as $x$ nudges slightly.</Variable>
    <Variable>**$f(x)$** — the function whose steepness we're measuring.</Variable>
    <Variable>**$x$** — the point along the curve where the derivative is evaluated.</Variable>
  </Variables>

  <Steps>
    <Step label="Differentiate this specific curve">
      For the curve above, $f(x) = x^2 - 4x + 5$. Differentiating term by term gives:

      $$
      \frac{df}{dx} = 2x - 4
      $$
    </Step>
    <Step label="Read the sign">
      Its sign tells you the direction:
      - positive means the curve rises as $x$ grows
      - negative means it falls
      - zero means you've hit a flat spot
    </Step>
  </Steps>
</Formalize>

<Play>
  <PlayDemo />

  Drag again — now watch the formula's value track the tangent line exactly. The derivative is the tangent's slope, nothing more.
</Play>

<WorkedExample>
  <Steps>
    <Step label="Evaluate the derivative at x = 5">
      $$
      f'(5) = 2(5) - 4 = 6
      $$
    </Step>
    <Step label="Read the sign">
      Positive, so the curve is rising there — to decrease $f$, move in the negative $x$ direction. This one fact —
      "the derivative points toward increase, so step against it" — is the entire idea behind gradient descent,
      several chapters from now.
    </Step>
  </Steps>
</WorkedExample>

<DerivativeCheckpoint />

<Summary>
  $$
  \frac{df}{dx}
  $$

  The derivative is the slope of the tangent line at a point: it points in the direction the function increases fastest, and it's exactly zero at a flat spot like a minimum. The next chapter generalizes this same idea to functions of several variables at once — the **gradient**.
</Summary>
