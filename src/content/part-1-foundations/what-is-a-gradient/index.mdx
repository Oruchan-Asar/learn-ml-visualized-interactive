import { Hook, Intuition, Formalize, Play, WorkedExample, Steps, Step, Summary } from "@/components/chapter/Beats";
import { IntuitionDemo, PlayDemo, GradientCheckpoint } from "./Demos";

<Hook>
  Which direction should you move along this curve to make it smaller — right now, without seeing the whole curve?
</Hook>

<Intuition>
  <IntuitionDemo />

  Drag the point along the curve. The dashed line is its steepness at that exact spot — tilt down to the right means the curve is falling there; tilt up means it's rising.
</Intuition>

<Formalize>
  That steepness has a name: the **gradient**. For a function of one variable, it's just the derivative —

  $$
  \nabla f(x) = \frac{df}{dx}
  $$

  For the curve above, $f(x) = x^2 - 4x + 5$. Differentiating term by term gives:

  $$
  \nabla f(x) = 2x - 4
  $$

  Its sign tells you the direction: positive means the curve rises as $x$ grows, negative means it falls.
</Formalize>

<Play>
  <PlayDemo />

  Drag again — now watch the formula's value track the tangent line exactly. The gradient is the tangent's slope, nothing more.
</Play>

<WorkedExample>
  <Steps>
    <Step label="Evaluate the gradient at x = 5">
      $$
      \nabla f(5) = 2(5) - 4 = 6
      $$
    </Step>
    <Step label="Read the sign">
      Positive, so the curve is rising there — to decrease $f$, move in the negative $x$ direction. This one fact —
      "the gradient points toward increase, so step against it" — is the entire idea behind gradient descent,
      several chapters from now.
    </Step>
  </Steps>
</WorkedExample>

<GradientCheckpoint />

<Summary>
  $$
  \nabla f(x) = \frac{df}{dx}
  $$

  The gradient is the slope of the tangent line at a point: it points in the direction the function increases fastest, and it's exactly zero at a flat spot like a minimum.
</Summary>
