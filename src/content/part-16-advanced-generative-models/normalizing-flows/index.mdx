<Hook>
  Part IV's GAN and Part X's VAE both generate samples from a simple noise source, but neither one tells
  you the exact probability of any particular output — a VAE only bounds it, a GAN doesn't define one at
  all. What if the generator were built so that density stayed exactly computable, every step of the way?
</Hook>

<Intuition>
  <IntuitionDemo />

  Drag $z$. The point traces out the density curve for $x=z^2$ — not because it's been fit to data, but
  because it's *derived*, exactly, from how much $f$ stretches space at that $z$.
</Intuition>

<Formalize>
  A **normalizing flow** builds $p_X$ from a trivial base density $p_Z$ by pushing it through an
  invertible, differentiable function $x = f(z)$. In one dimension, the change-of-variables formula says
  exactly how the density transforms:

  $$
  p_X(x) = \frac{p_Z\big(f^{-1}(x)\big)}{\left|\, df/dz \,\right|}, \qquad \text{evaluated at } z = f^{-1}(x)
  $$

  Wherever $f$ *stretches* space ($|df/dz|>1$), the same probability mass spreads over a wider region, so
  density there goes *down*. Wherever $f$ *compresses* space ($|df/dz|<1$), density goes *up*. Total
  probability is conserved — it's only ever redistributed, never created or destroyed — which is exactly
  what makes the density exactly computable instead of merely bounded.
</Formalize>

<Play>
  <PlayDemo />

  $Z\sim\text{Uniform}(0,1)$ starts perfectly flat. Squaring it compresses space near $z=0$ (small numbers
  get squeezed together even harder by squaring) and stretches space near $z=1$ — so density piles up near
  $x=0$ and thins out near $x=1$, with no information lost anywhere in between.
</Play>

<WorkedExample>
  The flow $x = z^2$, evaluated at two points:

  <Steps>
    <Step label="x = 0.25">
      $z = f^{-1}(0.25) = \sqrt{0.25} = 0.5$. $df/dz = 2(0.5) = 1$ — no stretching or compressing at all
      here. $p_X(0.25) = 1/1 = 1$, identical to the base density.
    </Step>
    <Step label="x = 0.04">
      $z = \sqrt{0.04} = 0.2$. $df/dz = 2(0.2) = 0.4$ — space is compressed to less than half its width
      here. $p_X(0.04) = 1/0.4 = 2.5$, two and a half times denser than the flat base.
    </Step>
  </Steps>
</WorkedExample>

<Summary>
  $$
  p_X(x) = \frac{p_Z(f^{-1}(x))}{|df/dz|}
  $$

  This chapter's flow was one fixed function on one variable, invertible by inspection. Real normalizing
  flows chain many learned, carefully-designed invertible layers (each engineered so its Jacobian
  determinant stays cheap to compute even in hundreds of dimensions) to turn a simple base distribution
  into something that can match real image or audio data — trading the VAE's approximate likelihood and
  the GAN's absent one for an exact number, at the cost of every layer having to stay invertible. The next
  chapter drops the invertibility requirement entirely and asks a different question: instead of an exact
  formula for the density, what if a model just learned which *direction* increases it?
</Summary>
