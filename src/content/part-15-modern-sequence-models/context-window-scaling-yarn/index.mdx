<Hook>
  A model trained on sequences up to length 8 gets handed a prompt of length 64. Every RoPE rotation angle
  past position 8 is one it has never seen produced during training. Retraining on longer sequences fixes
  it, but that's expensive — is there a way to reuse the same weights and just reinterpret the positions?
</Hook>

<Intuition>
  <IntuitionDemo />

  Push the scaling factor up and watch two numbers: the fast dimension's effective position never budges,
  but the slow dimension's collapses back down toward the trained range. The two frequencies aren't treated
  the same way at all — and that asymmetry is the entire idea.
</Intuition>

<Formalize>
  YaRN checks each RoPE frequency's *wavelength* — how many positions it takes to complete one full
  rotation — against the length the model was actually trained on, and only rescales the ones that need it:

  $$
  \text{pos}_{\text{eff}}(pos, i) = \begin{cases} pos / s & \text{wavelength}(i) > L \\ pos & \text{otherwise} \end{cases}
  $$

  <Variables>
    <Variable>**$pos$** — the token's true position in the (extended) sequence.</Variable>
    <Variable>**$i$** — which frequency this is; $\text{wavelength}(i) = 2\pi / \text{rate}(i)$.</Variable>
    <Variable>**$L$** — the original trained context length.</Variable>
    <Variable>**$s$** — the scaling factor: how many times longer the new context is meant to be.</Variable>
  </Variables>

  <Steps>
    <Step label="A fast dimension has already lapped the trained range many times">
      If its wavelength is shorter than $L$, it completed full rotations *inside* training. One more turn
      past $L$ looks, geometrically, exactly like turns it already learned from — safe to extrapolate as-is.
    </Step>
    <Step label="A slow dimension never even finished one turn during training">
      Its wavelength exceeds $L$, so every angle past $L$ is new territory. Dividing its position by $s$
      before rotating maps the *extended* range of positions back down into the angles the model actually
      trained on.
    </Step>
    <Step label="Real YaRN ramps smoothly between the two, this demo snaps">
      Production YaRN blends interpolation and extrapolation across a band of intermediate wavelengths so
      there's no hard seam. With only two frequencies here, the boundary is exact and hand-checkable — the
      idea is identical either way.
    </Step>
  </Steps>
</Formalize>

<Play>
  <PlayDemo />

  The dashed line is the fast dimension: flat, because it's never rescaled. The bold curve is the slow
  dimension's effective position at position 64 as the scaling factor slides from 1x (no rescaling — the
  raw, unsafe extrapolation) to 8x. By 8x it lands exactly on 8 — the length the model actually trained on.
</Play>

<WorkedExample>
  Trained context $L=8$; extended position $pos=64$ (an 8x-longer sequence); fast rate $=1$, slow rate
  $=0.01$ (so wavelengths $2\pi\approx6.28 < 8$ and $200\pi\approx628 > 8$):

  <Steps>
    <Step label="No scaling (s = 1): the slow dimension extrapolates raw">
      Angle $= 64 \times 0.01 = 0.64$ radians — over $9\times$ larger than any slow-dimension angle seen in
      training (the largest trained angle is $\approx 8\times0.01=0.08$).
    </Step>
    <Step label="Scale by 8x: divide position by 8 before rotating">
      Effective position $=64/8=8$. Angle $=8\times0.01=0.08$ — precisely the boundary angle the model
      trained up to.
    </Step>
    <Step label="The fast dimension is untouched throughout">
      Angle $=64\times1=64$ radians regardless of $s$ — it already completed more than 10 full turns inside
      the trained range, so extrapolating further changes nothing about how familiar the angle looks.
    </Step>
  </Steps>
</WorkedExample>

<Summary>
  $$
  \text{pos}_{\text{eff}}(pos, i) = \begin{cases} pos / s & \text{wavelength}(i) > L \\ pos & \text{otherwise} \end{cases}
  $$

  YaRN stretches a trained context window without touching a single weight, by rescaling *positions*
  rather than the model — and only for the RoPE frequencies whose wavelength is long enough that
  extrapolation would otherwise land them in angles never seen during training. Fast-rotating dimensions
  are left alone because they've already lapped the trained range many times over. It's a purely
  inference-time fix for the mismatch RoPE creates once a prompt runs longer than training — but it says
  nothing about the *cost* of actually processing that longer prompt, which is where the next chapter picks
  up.
</Summary>
