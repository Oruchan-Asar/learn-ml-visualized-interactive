<Hook>
  Retraining a language model to fix one wrong fact — it says Paris with total confidence, and you need
  it to say Rome — means touching every weight and risking every *other* fact it knows along the way.
  ROME's alternative: compute one closed-form update to one weight matrix that overwrites exactly that
  fact, provably leaving unrelated facts untouched.
</Hook>

<Intuition>
  <IntuitionDemo />

  At α = 0 the network still answers "capital of France" with Paris; at α = 1 it answers with Rome
  instead. Meanwhile "capital of Germany" never moves off Berlin, at either setting — because the edit
  reused a key vector that Germany's key happens to be orthogonal to.
</Intuition>

<Formalize>
  A fact lives in one weight matrix $W$ as a (key, value) association: $W \cdot k \approx v$. Editing it
  means finding the change $\Delta W$ that forces $W \cdot k$ onto a new target value $v^*$ exactly:

  $$
  \Delta W = \frac{(v^* - Wk)\,k^\top}{k^\top k}, \qquad W' = W + \Delta W
  $$

  <Variables>
    <Variable>**$W$** — the original weight matrix, mapping every subject's key to its object's value.</Variable>
    <Variable>**$k$** — the key vector for the one fact being edited (its subject).</Variable>
    <Variable>**$v^*$** — the new target value (its new object).</Variable>
    <Variable>**$\Delta W$** — the rank-1 update: an outer product of the needed residual with the key, scaled by $k^\top k$.</Variable>
  </Variables>

  <Steps>
    <Step label="It hits the target exactly">$W'k = Wk + \Delta W k = Wk + (v^*-Wk)\dfrac{k^\top k}{k^\top k} = v^*$.</Step>
    <Step label="It leaves orthogonal keys alone">For any other key $k_c$ with $k^\top k_c = 0$: $\Delta W k_c = (v^*-Wk)\dfrac{k^\top k_c}{k^\top k} = 0$, so $W'k_c = Wk_c$ — unchanged.</Step>
  </Steps>
</Formalize>

<Play>
  <PlayDemo />

  Drag α from 0 to 1: the France fact's Rome-retrieval score rises in a straight line from 0.25 to
  exactly 1.00, because the output itself is a linear interpolation between the old and new value. The
  Germany fact's own retrieval score sits at exactly 1.00 for every value of α — it was never touched.
</Play>

<WorkedExample>
  Editing "capital of France" from Paris to Rome, with $k_{\text{France}}=[1,0]$, $v_{\text{Paris}}=[2,1]$,
  $v_{\text{Rome}}=[0,4]$, and $W = \begin{bmatrix}2 & 0\\1 & 3\end{bmatrix}$:

  <Steps>
    <Step label="Current output for this key">$Wk_{\text{France}} = [2,1] = v_{\text{Paris}}$, as expected.</Step>
    <Step label="Residual">$v^* - Wk = [0,4]-[2,1] = [-2,3]$. And $k^\top k = 1$.</Step>
    <Step label="Rank-1 update">$\Delta W = [-2,3] \otimes [1,0] = \begin{bmatrix}-2 & 0\\3 & 0\end{bmatrix}$.</Step>
    <Step label="Edited matrix and check">
      $W' = \begin{bmatrix}0 & 0\\4 & 3\end{bmatrix}$. $W'k_{\text{France}} = [0,4] = v_{\text{Rome}}$ exactly.
      $W'k_{\text{Germany}} = [0,3] = v_{\text{Berlin}}$, untouched — since $k_{\text{France}}\cdot k_{\text{Germany}}=0$.
    </Step>
  </Steps>
</WorkedExample>

<ModelEditingCheckpoint />

<Summary>
  $$
  \Delta W = \frac{(v^* - Wk)\,k^\top}{k^\top k}
  $$

  One rank-1 update, computed once in closed form, overwrites a single (key, value) fact exactly while
  leaving every orthogonal key's mapping provably unchanged. ROME edits one fact this way per layer;
  MEMIT extends the same closed-form idea to update thousands of facts at once by solving for a shared
  update across many keys simultaneously — the mechanism this chapter traced by hand scales up, it
  doesn't change in kind.
</Summary>
