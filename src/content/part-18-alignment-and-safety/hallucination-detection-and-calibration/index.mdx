import { Hook, Intuition, Formalize, Play, WorkedExample, Steps, Step, Summary, Variables, Variable } from "@/components/chapter/Beats";
import { IntuitionDemo, PlayDemo, HallucinationCheckpoint } from "./Demos";

<Hook>
  A model that says "I'm 95% sure" and is wrong 5% of the time is calibrated and mostly harmless. A model
  that says "I'm 95% sure" and is wrong 40% of the time is confidently hallucinating — and the stated
  number alone can't tell you which one you're looking at until you check it against what actually
  happened.
</Hook>

<Intuition>
  <IntuitionDemo />

  Switch between the low and high threshold. A low bar trusts almost everything — including the one
  overconfident wrong answer sitting at 0.70. A high bar flags almost everything, including some
  genuinely correct answers that just weren't stated with top confidence. Neither extreme separates
  correct from wrong as cleanly as a threshold in between.
</Intuition>

<Formalize>
  Turn a stated confidence into a trust/flag decision with a single threshold, then measure how often
  that decision matches reality:

  $$
  \text{trust}(x) = \mathbb{1}[\text{confidence}(x) \ge t], \qquad \text{accuracy}(t) = \frac{1}{n}\sum_{x} \mathbb{1}[\text{trust}(x) = \text{correct}(x)]
  $$

  <Variables>
    <Variable>**$\text{confidence}(x)$** — the model's own stated probability that prediction $x$ is right.</Variable>
    <Variable>**$t$** — the threshold: trust anything at or above it, flag anything below.</Variable>
    <Variable>**$\text{correct}(x)$** — ground truth: was $x$ actually right?</Variable>
    <Variable>**$\text{accuracy}(t)$** — the fraction of predictions where the trust/flag call matched reality, at this threshold.</Variable>
  </Variables>

  <Steps>
    <Step label="Too low">A threshold below the overconfident wrong answer's 0.70 confidence trusts it — a miss.</Step>
    <Step label="Too high">A threshold above the lowest genuinely correct answer's 0.85 confidence flags it too — also a miss.</Step>
    <Step label="Just right">Anywhere strictly above 0.70 and up to 0.85, every one of the seven predictions is classified correctly.</Step>
  </Steps>
</Formalize>

<Play>
  <PlayDemo />

  Drag the threshold up from 0: accuracy rises, peaks at a perfect 7/7 for any value between the
  hallucination's 0.70 and the lowest genuinely correct answer's 0.85, then falls again as the threshold
  climbs past 0.85 and starts flagging correct answers too.
</Play>

<WorkedExample>
  Checking threshold $t=0.75$ against all seven predictions:

  <Steps>
    <Step label="The three confident, correct ones">
      - $0.95 \ge 0.75$ → trusted, actually correct → match
      - $0.90 \ge 0.75$ → trusted, actually correct → match
      - $0.85 \ge 0.75$ → trusted, actually correct → match
    </Step>
    <Step label="The overconfident hallucination">0.70 does not clear 0.75 → flagged, and it's actually wrong. A match — exactly the case a lower threshold would have missed.</Step>
    <Step label="The three lower-confidence wrong ones">
      - $0.50 < 0.75$ → flagged, actually wrong → match
      - $0.35 < 0.75$ → flagged, actually wrong → match
      - $0.15 < 0.75$ → flagged, actually wrong → match
    </Step>
    <Step label="Total">7 out of 7 — perfect separation, for this dataset, at this threshold.</Step>
  </Steps>
</WorkedExample>

<HallucinationCheckpoint />

<Summary>
  $$
  \text{accuracy}(t) = \frac{1}{n}\sum_x \mathbb{1}\big[\mathbb{1}[\text{confidence}(x)\ge t] = \text{correct}(x)\big]
  $$

  A single threshold on stated confidence is only as good as the gap between a model's well-calibrated
  cluster and its overconfident mistakes — when a hallucination's confidence sits inside that gap
  instead of below it, no threshold can catch it, and detecting it needs a signal confidence alone
  doesn't carry, such as consistency across resampled answers or a probe on the model's internal states.
</Summary>
