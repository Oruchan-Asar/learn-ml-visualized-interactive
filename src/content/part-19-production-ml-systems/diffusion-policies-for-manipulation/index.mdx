<Hook>
  The last chapter's VLA policy output one action at a time: an instruction goes in, a single delta comes
  out. A diffusion policy does something structurally different — it outputs the robot's *entire* future
  trajectory in one shot, as a sequence of waypoints, by starting from pure noise and denoising toward a
  smooth path. That's also how it captures multi-modality: two equally valid ways to reach for a mug (from
  the left, or from the right) are two different clean trajectories the same noisy start could denoise
  toward.
</Hook>

<Intuition>
  <IntuitionDemo />

  Three arrows, three target dots. Each denoising step slides every arrow a fixed fraction of the way from
  where it started toward its target — press the button enough times and the noisy scatter becomes the
  smooth path underneath it.
</Intuition>

<Formalize>
  At denoising step $t$ out of $T$ total steps, every waypoint sits at a fixed fraction of the way from its
  noisy start to its clean target:

  $$
  w_t = w_{\text{noise}} + \frac{t}{T}\left(w_{\text{clean}} - w_{\text{noise}}\right)
  $$

  <Variables>
    <Variable>**$w_t$** — one waypoint's position after $t$ denoising steps.</Variable>
    <Variable>**$w_{\text{noise}}$** — that waypoint's pure-noise starting position, at $t=0$.</Variable>
    <Variable>**$w_{\text{clean}}$** — that waypoint's position in the clean trajectory the policy was trained to produce, reached at $t=T$.</Variable>
    <Variable>**$t / T$** — how far through the denoising schedule this step is, from $0$ (all noise) to $1$ (fully clean).</Variable>
  </Variables>

  Real diffusion and flow-matching policies replace this fixed linear path with one predicted step-by-step
  by a trained network, conditioned on the current noisy trajectory and the observation — but every step
  still moves a fixed amount from wherever the trajectory currently is toward wherever the network
  believes "clean" looks like.

  <Steps>
    <Step label="Every waypoint denoises independently, at the same rate">
      Because $t/T$ is shared across all waypoints, they all close the same fraction of their own
      individual gap at each step — that's why the whole scattered set visibly tightens into a path
      together, rather than one point arriving first.
    </Step>
    <Step label="More steps trade compute for a cleaner trajectory">
      $t=0$ is unusable noise; $t=T$ is exact. Real policies pick $T$ as the smallest step count that still
      lands close enough to a trajectory worth executing.
    </Step>
  </Steps>
</Formalize>

<Play>
  <PlayDemo />

  The distance from clean drops from roughly 15 units at pure noise to exactly 0 at the final step — not
  linearly in distance, since each *waypoint* closes a fixed fraction of its own gap, and those gaps
  started at different sizes.
</Play>

<WorkedExample>
  <Steps>
    <Step label="The middle waypoint's noise and clean positions">
      Noise: $(3, -3)$. Clean: $(2, 2)$.
    </Step>
    <Step label="At step 3 of 4, t/T = 0.75">
      $w_{0.75} = (3,-3) + 0.75\big((2,2)-(3,-3)\big) = (3,-3) + 0.75(-1, 5)$.
    </Step>
    <Step label="Compute each coordinate">
      $x = 3 + 0.75(-1) = 2.25$. $y = -3 + 0.75(5) = 0.75$. The waypoint lands at exactly $(2.25, 0.75)$ —
      three-quarters of the way from noise to clean.
    </Step>
  </Steps>
</WorkedExample>

<Summary>
  $$
  w_t = w_{\text{noise}} + \frac{t}{T}\left(w_{\text{clean}} - w_{\text{noise}}\right)
  $$

  A diffusion policy turns "predict the next action" into "denoise a whole trajectory," which is what lets
  it represent genuinely multi-modal behavior and produce smooth, temporally consistent motion instead of
  a jittery sequence of independent single-step guesses. The trajectory this chapter denoised was still
  produced entirely in simulation-perfect math — the next chapter asks what happens when a policy trained
  this cleanly meets a real actuator that doesn't behave quite the way the simulator promised.
</Summary>
