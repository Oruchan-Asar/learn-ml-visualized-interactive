<Hook>
  A dashboard that only tracks accuracy will tell you a model is fine right up until the moment it isn't.
  That's not a bug in the dashboard — it's a real blind spot. Accuracy can average out a distribution shift
  so completely that the two numbers are identical to the decimal.
</Hook>

<Intuition>
  <IntuitionDemo />

  Switch snapshots. The bars visibly reshape — traffic that used to sit mostly in the medium bin now spreads
  across all three — while the accuracy readout barely twitches.
</Intuition>

<Formalize>
  Split inputs into bins, each with its own fixed accuracy $a_i$. Overall accuracy is those accuracies
  weighted by how often each bin occurs:

  $$
  \text{accuracy} = \sum_i P(\text{bin}_i)\, a_i
  $$

  <Variables>
    <Variable>**$\text{accuracy}$** — the overall model accuracy on live traffic, averaged across bins.</Variable>
    <Variable>**$i$** — index ranging over each bin the inputs are split into.</Variable>
    <Variable>**$P(\text{bin}_i)$** — the fraction of inputs that fall into bin $i$.</Variable>
    <Variable>**$a_i$** — the fixed accuracy the model gets on inputs in bin $i$.</Variable>
  </Variables>

  A **drift score** measures the distributions directly, independent of
  what accuracy happens to do:

  $$
  \text{drift} = D_{KL}(P_{\text{train}} \,\|\, P_{\text{live}})
  $$

  <Variables>
    <Variable>**$\text{drift}$** — the drift score: how far the live input distribution has moved from the training one.</Variable>
    <Variable>**$D_{KL}(\cdot \Vert \cdot)$** — the **KL divergence**, short for **Kullback–Leibler divergence**: the extra bits it costs to describe one distribution using a code built for the other.</Variable>
    <Variable>**$P_{\text{train}}$** — the distribution of inputs the model was trained and validated on.</Variable>
    <Variable>**$P_{\text{live}}$** — the distribution of inputs the model is actually seeing in production right now.</Variable>
  </Variables>

  <Steps>
    <Step label="Accuracy can hide real change">
      Two very different bin distributions can land on the exact same weighted sum in the accuracy formula
      above — a coincidence of arithmetic, not evidence that nothing changed.
    </Step>
    <Step label="Drift alerts independent of accuracy">
      Above a chosen threshold, the input distribution has moved enough to alert on — regardless of whether
      accuracy noticed.
    </Step>
  </Steps>
</Formalize>

<Play>
  <PlayDemo />

  Same accuracy, four significant digits deep. A completely different drift score. One of these two numbers
  is actually watching the thing that changed.
</Play>

<WorkedExample>
  <Steps>
    <Step label="Training distribution: 20% low, 50% medium, 30% high">
      Weighted accuracy: $0.2(0.95) + 0.5(0.85) + 0.3(0.75) = 0.19 + 0.425 + 0.225 = 0.84$.
    </Step>
    <Step label="Live distribution: 30% low, 30% medium, 40% high">
      Weighted accuracy: $0.3(0.95) + 0.3(0.85) + 0.4(0.75) = 0.285 + 0.255 + 0.3 = 0.84$ — identical, to the
      hundredth.
    </Step>
    <Step label="The distributions are not identical">
      $D_{KL}(P_{\text{train}} \| P_{\text{live}}) = \sum_i P_{\text{train}}(i)\log_2\frac{P_{\text{train}}(i)}{P_{\text{live}}(i)}$:

      - Low: $0.2\log_2(0.2/0.3) \approx 0.2(-0.585) \approx -0.117$
      - Medium: $0.5\log_2(0.5/0.3) \approx 0.5(0.737) \approx 0.368$
      - High: $0.3\log_2(0.3/0.4) \approx 0.3(-0.415) \approx -0.125$

      Sum: $-0.117+0.368-0.125\approx0.127$ bits — well past a 0.05-bit alert threshold.

      - The medium bin's share fell from 50% to 30% (a 40% relative drop).
      - The high bin's share rose from 30% to 40% (a one-third relative gain).

      Accuracy never had a chance to see it, because the per-bin accuracies happened to average out.
    </Step>
  </Steps>
</WorkedExample>

<Summary>
  $$
  \text{accuracy} = \sum_i P(\text{bin}_i)\, a_i \qquad \text{drift} = D_{KL}(P_{\text{train}} \,\|\, P_{\text{live}})
  $$

  Accuracy is a lossy summary — it compresses a whole distribution of behavior into one number, and lossy
  compression can hide exactly the kind of change that matters. A drift score doesn't replace accuracy
  monitoring; it watches something accuracy structurally cannot: whether the inputs still look like the ones
  the model was validated on. Real feature stores and monitoring systems track both, because either one alone
  eventually gets fooled. The next chapter turns from watching a single model in production to training one
  across many machines at once.
</Summary>
