import { IntuitionDemo, PlayDemo, TwoPCCheckpoint } from "./Demos";

<Hook>
  A bank transfer touches two databases at once — debit one account, credit another. Either both
  happen or neither does. But the two databases live on different machines, and either one might
  say no. How do you make "all or nothing" hold across a network?
</Hook>

<Intuition>
  <IntuitionDemo />

  Step through the trace. A coordinator first asks every participant to vote — nobody commits
  anything yet, they only promise they *could*. Only after all three votes are in does the
  coordinator tell everyone what actually happened.
</Intuition>

<Formalize>
  $$
  \text{decision} = \begin{cases} \text{commit} & \text{if every participant voted yes} \\ \text{abort} & \text{otherwise} \end{cases}
  $$

  <Variables>
    <Variable>**Coordinator** — the single node that collects votes and announces the decision.</Variable>
    <Variable>**Participant** — a node holding one piece of the transaction, able to vote yes or no.</Variable>
    <Variable>**Phase 1 (voting)** — coordinator asks, each participant votes yes or no.</Variable>
    <Variable>**Phase 2 (commit)** — coordinator broadcasts the decision; participants ack.</Variable>
  </Variables>

  <Steps>
    <Step label="One no outvotes any number of yeses">
      The rule is a logical AND across all votes, not a majority — a single "no" from any one
      participant forces every other participant, even ones that voted yes, to abort.
    </Step>
    <Step label="A yes vote is a promise, not an action">
      Voting yes means "I could commit if told to" — the participant holds its locks and waits. It
      cannot act until phase 2 tells it what the group decided.
    </Step>
    <Step label="The blocking problem">
      If the coordinator crashes after collecting all-yes votes but before broadcasting the
      decision, a yes-voting participant is stuck: it cannot guess commit (some other participant
      might have voted no) or guess abort (everyone else might commit) — it can only wait.
    </Step>
  </Steps>
</Formalize>

<Play>
  <PlayDemo />

  Flip P2 to NO. Watch the phase-2 messages: the coordinator now sends GLOBAL-ABORT to *every*
  participant, including P1 and P3, who both voted yes. Their yes vote bought them nothing — the
  group decision overrides it. Now set all three back to YES and crash the coordinator — every
  participant is holding its locks with no way to know what to do next.
</Play>

<WorkedExample>
  Three participants, P1 and P3 vote yes, P2 votes no.

  <Steps>
    <Step label="Phase 1 — collect votes">
      Coordinator sends VOTE-REQUEST to P1, P2, P3. Votes come back:

      - P1 = YES
      - P2 = NO
      - P3 = YES
    </Step>
    <Step label="Apply the decision rule">
      Not every vote was yes ($P2 = \text{no}$), so the AND fails: decision = abort.
    </Step>
    <Step label="Phase 2 — broadcast and ack">
      Coordinator sends GLOBAL-ABORT to all three, including P1 and P3. All three ack. Total
      messages: 3 requests + 3 votes + 3 decisions + 3 acks = 12, exactly as many as the all-yes
      case — the number of messages never depends on the outcome.
    </Step>
  </Steps>
</WorkedExample>

<TwoPCCheckpoint />

<Summary>
  $$
  \text{decision} = \bigwedge_{i} (v_i = \text{yes})
  $$

  2PC guarantees atomicity — commit or abort happens everywhere, never a mix — by making every
  participant wait for the coordinator's single, final word. That same waiting is its weakness: if
  the coordinator disappears after collecting all-yes votes, every participant is blocked holding
  its locks until it comes back. The next chapter adds a phase specifically to remove that single
  point of blocking.
</Summary>
