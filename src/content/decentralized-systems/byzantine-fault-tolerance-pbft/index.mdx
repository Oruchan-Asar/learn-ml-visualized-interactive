<Hook>
  Raft assumes a crashed node just goes quiet. What if a node doesn't crash — it keeps running, but sends
  *different* answers to different peers, or votes for two conflicting values at once? A simple majority no
  longer protects you.
</Hook>

<Formalize>
  **PBFT** tolerates $f$ simultaneously Byzantine replicas — ones that can lie, equivocate, or stay silent —
  out of a cluster of $N$, as long as:

  $$
  N \geq 3f + 1
  $$

  Every request then goes through three phases, each requiring a replica to collect a **quorum of $2f+1$
  matching votes** before it moves on:

  <Steps>
    <Step label="Phase 1 — Pre-prepare">
      The primary proposes a value to every backup: `PRE-PREPARE(value)`.
    </Step>
    <Step label="Phase 2 — Prepare">
      Every replica that accepts the proposal broadcasts `PREPARE(value)` to every other replica. A replica is
      **prepared** once $2f+1$ replicas (including itself) report the same value.
    </Step>
    <Step label="Phase 3 — Commit">
      Once prepared, a replica broadcasts `COMMIT(value)`. It **commits** once $2f+1$ replicas report the same
      value here too.
    </Step>
  </Steps>

  <Variables>
    <Variable>**$f$** — the number of Byzantine replicas the cluster must survive.</Variable>
    <Variable>**$N = 3f+1$** — the minimum cluster size that guarantees safety against $f$ liars.</Variable>
    <Variable>**quorum $= 2f+1$** — matching votes a replica needs each phase before advancing.</Variable>
  </Variables>

  The $3f+1$ bound isn't arbitrary: any two quorums of size $2f+1$ out of $N$ overlap in at least $2(2f+1) - N$
  replicas. With $N = 3f+1$, that overlap is $f+1$ — one more than the number of liars — so no matter which $f$
  replicas are Byzantine, at least one *honest* replica is forced to sit in every pair of quorums. Drop below
  $3f+1$ and that guarantee disappears.
</Formalize>

<WorkedExample>
  4 replicas — R0 (primary), R1, R2, R3 — tolerating $f=1$. R3 is Byzantine.

  <Steps>
    <Step label="Pre-prepare">
      R0 sends `PRE-PREPARE(op-42)` to R1, R2, R3.
    </Step>
    <Step label="Prepare votes come in">
      R0, R1, and R2 all broadcast `PREPARE(op-42)` — three matching votes. R3 broadcasts `PREPARE(op-fake)`
      instead, equivocating.
    </Step>
    <Step label="Check the PREPARE quorum">
      `countMatchingVotes` counts 3 replicas reporting `op-42`. `quorumSize(1) = 2(1)+1 = 3`. Three matches meets
      quorum — R3's mismatched vote simply doesn't count, and doesn't need to.
    </Step>
    <Step label="Commit votes, and the final check">
      R0, R1, R2 broadcast `COMMIT(op-42)`; R3 sends nothing at all this round. That's still 3 matching commits —
      quorum again — so `runPbftRound` reports `committed: true`. One lying replica changed nothing.
    </Step>
  </Steps>
</WorkedExample>

<Summary>
  Byzantine fault tolerance costs more than crash tolerance: $3f+1$ replicas instead of $2f+1$, and a $2f+1$
  quorum every phase instead of a plain majority. What you buy with that cost is a guarantee that survives active
  lying, not just silence — any $f$ Byzantine replicas can never stop an honest quorum from agreeing, and can
  never trick two honest replicas into committing different values.
</Summary>
