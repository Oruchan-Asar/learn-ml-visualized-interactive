<Hook>
  Two replicas of the same counter each accept writes while offline, then reconnect in some
  unpredictable order — no coordinator, no locking, maybe not even in the same order twice. Is
  there a data structure where "just apply whatever you both have" is *guaranteed* to produce the
  same answer, no matter the order?
</Hook>

<Intuition>
  <IntuitionDemo />

  Click P and Q's buttons in whatever order and however many times you like — they never talk to
  each other while doing it. Then hit "Merge": both boxes turn out exactly the same, holding the
  full total of everything either one ever did.
</Intuition>

<Formalize>
  A **G-Counter** (grow-only counter) CRDT gives every replica its own slot that only *that*
  replica may increment. Merging two replicas' states takes the pointwise max of every slot:

  $$
  \text{merge}(A, B)_i = \max(A_i,\, B_i)
  $$

  <Variables>
    <Variable>**$A_i$**, **$B_i$** — replica $i$'s slot count as last seen by state $A$ or $B$.</Variable>
    <Variable>**$\text{merge}(A,B)_i$** — the merged state's slot $i$: whichever of $A$ or $B$ has seen more of replica $i$'s increments.</Variable>
    <Variable>**value(state)** — the counter's logical total: the sum of every slot.</Variable>
  </Variables>

  <Steps>
    <Step label="Merge is commutative and idempotent">
      $\max(A_i,B_i) = \max(B_i,A_i)$ for every slot, so it never matters who initiates a merge —
      and merging a state with itself changes nothing, so re-merging the same update twice is
      harmless.
    </Step>
    <Step label="Merge is associative">
      $\max$ over three or more values doesn't depend on how they're grouped, so merging replicas
      two at a time, in any order or pairing, reaches the exact same final state as merging them
      all at once.
    </Step>
  </Steps>
</Formalize>

<Play>
  <PlayDemo />

  Increment P and Q in any pattern — three P's then one Q, alternating, whatever. The panel always
  computes merge(P, Q) *and* merge(Q, P) side by side: they never disagree, at any point, no
  matter what either replica has done up to that moment.
</Play>

<WorkedExample>
  Three replicas, each incrementing only their own slot, entirely independently:

  <Steps>
    <Step label="Independent local states">
      - P increments 3 times: $\{P{:}3, Q{:}0, R{:}0\}$
      - Q increments 2 times: $\{P{:}0, Q{:}2, R{:}0\}$
      - R increments once: $\{P{:}0, Q{:}0, R{:}1\}$

      None of them has seen either of the others' updates.
    </Step>
    <Step label="Merging P and Q, either direction">
      $\text{merge}(P,Q) = \{P{:}\max(3,0), Q{:}\max(0,2), R{:}\max(0,0)\} = \{3, 2, 0\}$. Merging
      the other way, $\text{merge}(Q,P)$, produces the exact same $\{3, 2, 0\}$.
    </Step>
    <Step label="Bringing in R, in either grouping">
      $\text{merge}(\text{merge}(P,Q), R) = \{P{:}\max(3,0), Q{:}\max(2,0), R{:}\max(0,1)\} = \{3,2,1\}$,
      and $\text{merge}(P, \text{merge}(Q,R))$ also comes out to $\{3,2,1\}$ — total value $6$,
      regardless of which two replicas happened to sync with each other first.
    </Step>
  </Steps>
</WorkedExample>

<CrdtCheckpoint />

<Summary>
  $$
  \text{merge}(A,B)_i = \max(A_i, B_i)
  $$

  A CRDT's merge rule is built so that commutativity, associativity, and idempotence hold by
  construction — not by careful protocol design, but because $\max$ (or union, for other CRDTs)
  simply has those properties. That's what "no coordination" really buys: replicas can accept
  writes and sync with whichever peers happen to be reachable, in whatever order, and still be
  mathematically guaranteed to converge to one shared state. The next chapter's Dynamo-style store
  reaches for a different tool — vector clocks — for exactly the writes a CRDT's merge rule
  *can't* silently resolve on its own.
</Summary>
