<Hook>
  Ridge regression shrinks correlated predictors toward each other, but it never drops one entirely — no
  matter how large the penalty, both weights stay some nonzero decimal. What would it take for a
  regularizer to actually decide "this predictor isn't worth keeping" and set its weight to *exactly* zero?
</Hook>

<Intuition>
  <IntuitionDemo />

  Same two nearly-identical predictors as the ridge chapter. Push λ up and, past a small threshold, $w_2$
  doesn't just shrink toward zero the way ridge's would — it lands exactly on it and stays there.
</Intuition>

<Formalize>
  Lasso swaps ridge's squared penalty for an absolute-value one:

  $$
  \text{MSE}(\mathbf{w}) + \lambda \sum_j |w_j|
  $$

  <Variables>
    <Variable>**MSE($\mathbf{w}$)** — mean squared error (lasso conventionally penalizes the *averaged* error, not the raw sum ridge uses — so the same numeric λ lands at a different effective strength for each).</Variable>
    <Variable>**$\lambda$** — the penalty strength, same role as in ridge.</Variable>
    <Variable>**$|w_j|$** — the absolute value of the $j$-th weight — the source of everything that follows.</Variable>
  </Variables>

  Unlike ridge, this loss has no closed form — it's minimized by cyclic **coordinate descent**: repeatedly
  pick one weight, hold the others fixed, and solve for it via **soft-thresholding**,
  $\text{soft}(\rho, \gamma) = \text{sign}(\rho)\max(|\rho|-\gamma, 0)$.

  <Steps>
    <Step label="A squared penalty is smooth at zero; an absolute-value one has a corner">
      $w^2$ has zero slope at $w=0$, so shrinking a weight that's already small barely pulls it further.
      $|w|$ has a constant-slope *kink* at $w=0$ — that corner is what a coordinate update can land exactly on.
    </Step>
    <Step label="Soft-thresholding is the algebraic form of that corner">
      Solving the 1D optimality condition for one weight at a time gives exactly the soft-threshold rule:
      if the weight's "signal" $\rho$ is smaller in magnitude than $\gamma$, the optimal weight is precisely
      $0$ — not close to it.
    </Step>
    <Step label="Correlated predictors can make lasso's choice unstable">
      When two predictors carry almost the same signal, which one gets zeroed can depend on arbitrary
      details like update order — a real quirk of lasso under collinearity, and the reason ElasticNet
      exists (next chapter).
    </Step>
  </Steps>
</Formalize>

  <PlayDemo />

  Same λ, fed to both penalties on the same data. Lasso routinely shows a flat $0.000$ for $w_2$; ridge,
  right below it, never does — its $w_2$ is always some nonzero decimal, however small.
</Play>

<WorkedExample>
  Same toy setup as ridge: $x_1=[1,2,3,4]$, $x_2=[1,2,3,4.5]$ (correlation $\approx 0.998$), $y=[2,4,7,8]$.

  <Steps>
    <Step label="Unregularized, lasso agrees with OLS">
      At $\lambda=0$, coordinate descent converges to the same unstable fit as before: $w_1\approx3.929$,
      $w_2\approx-1.714$.
    </Step>
    <Step label="A tiny penalty already zeroes w2 (λ = 0.03)">
      By $\lambda=0.03$, soft-thresholding has pushed $w_2$'s "signal" inside the dead zone: $w_2$ becomes
      exactly $0$, while $w_1\approx2.096$ absorbs essentially the whole fit.
    </Step>
    <Step label="It stays exactly zero across a wide range (λ = 0.5)">
      At $\lambda=0.5$: $w_1\approx2.033$, $w_2=0$ — lasso has picked predictor 1 and permanently discarded
      predictor 2, a form of automatic feature selection ridge structurally cannot do.
    </Step>
  </Steps>
</WorkedExample>

<LassoCheckpoint />

<Summary>
  $$
  \text{MSE}(\mathbf{w}) + \lambda \sum_j |w_j|
  $$

  Lasso's $|w_j|$ penalty has a corner at zero that ridge's smooth $w_j^2$ doesn't — and coordinate-wise
  optimization tends to land exactly on that corner. The result is automatic feature selection: past a
  threshold λ, a weak predictor's weight isn't just small, it's *exactly* $0$.
</Summary>
