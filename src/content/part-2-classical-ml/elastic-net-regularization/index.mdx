import { IntuitionDemo, PlayDemo, ElasticNetCheckpoint } from "./Demos";

<Hook>
  Lasso picks one of two nearly-identical predictors and throws the other away — but *which* one it keeps
  can be an arbitrary accident of the data, not a meaningful choice. What if you wanted the sparsity of
  lasso, but without that fragile all-or-nothing bet on correlated features?
</Hook>

<Intuition>
  <IntuitionDemo />

  Same two correlated predictors as the last two chapters, same total penalty strength — only the *mix*
  between L1 and L2 changes. At α=1 it's pure lasso: $w_2$ sits at exact zero. Slide α down and watch $w_2$
  revive, smoothly, long before you reach pure ridge.
</Intuition>

<Formalize>
  ElasticNet's penalty is a weighted blend of both:

  $$
  \text{MSE}(\mathbf{w}) + \lambda\left[\alpha \sum_j |w_j| \;+\; \frac{1-\alpha}{2}\sum_j w_j^2\right]
  $$

  <Variables>
    <Variable>**$\lambda$** — the total penalty strength, same role as in ridge and lasso.</Variable>
    <Variable>**$\alpha$** — the mixing parameter, between 0 and 1: $\alpha=1$ is pure lasso, $\alpha=0$ is pure ridge.</Variable>
    <Variable>**$\sum_j |w_j|$, $\sum_j w_j^2$** — the L1 and L2 penalty terms from the last two chapters, now combined.</Variable>
  </Variables>

  <Steps>
    <Step label="Each coordinate update generalizes both single-penalty rules">
      Coordinate descent still soft-thresholds by the L1 share of the penalty, then divides by the data
      term *plus* the L2 share — set $\alpha=1$ and the L2 share vanishes, recovering lasso's update
      exactly; set $\alpha=0$ and the soft-threshold cutoff vanishes, recovering a ridge-like update.
    </Step>
    <Step label="The L2 term restores ridge's grouping effect">
      Even a small amount of L2 ($\alpha$ just under 1) is enough to stop lasso's arbitrary all-or-nothing
      choice between correlated predictors — both stay in the model, sharing the fit instead of one
      evicting the other.
    </Step>
    <Step label="The L1 term keeps sparsity available">
      Push $\alpha$ close enough to 1 and the sparsity comes back — ElasticNet doesn't have to choose
      between "shrinks everything" and "zeros out weak predictors"; $\alpha$ dials between them.
    </Step>
  </Steps>
</Formalize>

<Play>
  <PlayDemo />

  Drag α down from 1. Somewhere before you reach 0, $w_2$ stops being exactly zero and starts climbing —
  that's the L2 term's grouping effect outweighing L1's corner.
</Play>

<WorkedExample>
  Same toy setup as ridge and lasso: $x_1=[1,2,3,4]$, $x_2=[1,2,3,4.5]$, $y=[2,4,7,8]$, fixed $\lambda=0.5$.

  <Steps>
    <Step label="α = 1 (pure lasso): sparse, as expected">
      $w_1 \approx 2.033$, $w_2 = 0$ exactly — identical to the lasso chapter's own result at this λ.
    </Step>
    <Step label="α = 0.5: both predictors survive, close together">
      $w_1 \approx 1.069$, $w_2 \approx 0.902$ — no longer sparse, but the two nearly-collinear predictors
      are now sharing credit almost the way ridge would.
    </Step>
    <Step label="α = 0 (pure ridge): fully grouped">
      $w_1 \approx 1.028$, $w_2 \approx 0.941$ — the two weights are about as close together as ridge alone
      would produce (a differently-scaled λ than the closed-form ridge chapter, since this loss averages
      the squared error instead of summing it — but the qualitative grouping behavior matches).
    </Step>
  </Steps>
</WorkedExample>

<ElasticNetCheckpoint />

<Summary>
  $$
  \text{MSE}(\mathbf{w}) + \lambda\left[\alpha \sum_j |w_j| + \frac{1-\alpha}{2}\sum_j w_j^2\right]
  $$

  ElasticNet doesn't pick a side between L1 and L2 — it interpolates. Lasso's sparsity stays available
  near $\alpha=1$, and even a little L2 mixed in restores ridge's grouping effect on correlated predictors,
  fixing the exact fragility that pure lasso has no way to fix on its own.
</Summary>
