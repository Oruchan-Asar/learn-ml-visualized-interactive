<Hook>
  No labels this time — just a scatter of points. Can an algorithm find the groups on its own?
</Hook>

<Intuition>
  <IntuitionDemo />

  Drag the two diamond centroids anywhere, then take a step. Every point turns the color of whichever centroid it's closest to; then each centroid jumps to the middle of the points that just turned its color. Do that a few times and the centroids stop moving — they've found the two blobs, starting from nothing but a guess.
</Intuition>

<Formalize>
  This is **k-means**, and one iteration is just two moves repeated:

  $$
  \text{assign: } c_i = \arg\min_k \lVert x_i - \mu_k \rVert^2 \qquad\qquad \text{update: } \mu_k = \frac{1}{|S_k|}\sum_{i \in S_k} x_i
  $$

  <Variables>
    <Variable>**$c_i$** — the cluster that point $i$ gets assigned to.</Variable>
    <Variable>**$x_i$** — the coordinates of point $i$.</Variable>
    <Variable>**$\mu_k$** — the centroid (mean position) of cluster $k$.</Variable>
    <Variable>**$S_k$** — the set of points currently assigned to cluster $k$.</Variable>
  </Variables>

  <Steps>
    <Step label="Assign, then update">
      Assign every point to its nearest centroid, then move each centroid to the mean of the points now assigned
      to it.
    </Step>
    <Step label="Assignment alone stalls">
      Assignment without updating just repeats the same bad guess forever.
    </Step>
    <Step label="Updating alone misses reassignments">
      And updating without reassigning never notices when a point should switch sides.
    </Step>
  </Steps>
</Formalize>

<Play>
  <PlayDemo />

  Try a few very different starting positions for the centroids — both inside one blob, both in empty space, swapped to the wrong side entirely. The path each takes to get there differs, but for two well-separated blobs like these, they all end up in the same place.
</Play>

<WorkedExample>
  Starting both centroids in empty space — one at $(9,1)$, the other at $(1,8)$:

  <Steps>
    <Step label="First assignment step is messy">
      Point $(2,1)$ ends up nearer to $(9,1)$ (distance $7$) than to $(1,8)$ (distance $\approx 7.07$) — a near-tie
      that could easily have gone the other way.
    </Step>
    <Step label="First update already helps a lot">
      That first assignment sorts 6 points to each centroid: $(9,1)$'s group ends up
      $\{(2,1),(8,7),(9,7),(9,8),(8.5,7.5),(9.5,8)\}$, and $(1,8)$'s group gets the other six. Averaging
      each group's coordinates:
      - $(9,1)$'s new centroid: $x=(2+8+9+9+8.5+9.5)/6=46/6\approx7.67$, $y=(1+7+7+8+7.5+8)/6=38.5/6\approx6.42$
      - $(1,8)$'s new centroid: $x=(1+1+2+1.5+2.5+8)/6=16/6\approx2.67$, $y=(1+2+2+1.5+2+8)/6=16.5/6=2.75$

      — close enough to the real blobs that the *next* assignment step cleanly sorts every point correctly.
    </Step>
    <Step label="Converges to the true means">
      With the centroids that close, every point sorts to its own blob this time — no more near-ties.
      Averaging each true blob's 6 points:
      - Blob near $(9,1)$: $x=(8+9+8+9+8.5+9.5)/6=52/6\approx8.67$, $y=(7+7+8+8+7.5+8)/6=45.5/6\approx7.58$
      - Blob near $(1,8)$: $x=(1+2+1+2+1.5+2.5)/6=10/6\approx1.67$, $y=(1+1+2+2+1.5+2)/6=9.5/6\approx1.58$

      — landing exactly on both blobs' true means, and a further step changes nothing at all.
    </Step>
  </Steps>
</WorkedExample>

<Summary>
  $$
  \text{assign: } c_i = \arg\min_k \lVert x_i - \mu_k \rVert^2 \qquad\qquad \text{update: } \mu_k = \frac{1}{|S_k|}\sum_{i \in S_k} x_i
  $$

  K-means needs no labels at all — it only ever looks at distances between points and centroids. That's also its limit: it can only find clusters shaped like blobs around a center. The next chapter's technique looks at the data differently, by asking which *directions* it actually varies in.
</Summary>
