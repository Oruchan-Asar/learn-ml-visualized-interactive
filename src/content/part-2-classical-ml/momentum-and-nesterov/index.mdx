<Hook>
  Momentum reacts to the gradient right where it's standing, then carries that reaction forward as velocity.
  What if, instead, it looked ahead to roughly where the velocity is about to carry it — and reacted to
  *that* gradient instead?
</Hook>

<Intuition>
  <IntuitionDemo />

  Same ravine, same starting point, same β. The accent trail is plain Momentum; the ink trail is Nesterov.
  Step through it — the two are identical on step one, since velocity starts at zero either way, and then
  they visibly split.
</Intuition>

<Formalize>
  Momentum evaluates the gradient at the current point. **Nesterov accelerated gradient (NAG)** evaluates it
  at a look-ahead point first:

  $$
  \tilde\theta = \theta - \eta\beta v, \qquad v' = \beta v + \nabla f(\tilde\theta), \qquad \theta' = \theta - \eta v'
  $$

  <Variables>
    <Variable>**$\tilde\theta$** — the look-ahead point: where momentum alone would carry $\theta$ before this step's gradient is even computed.</Variable>
    <Variable>**$v$** — the velocity carried over from the previous step.</Variable>
    <Variable>**$v'$** — the new velocity, built from the gradient at $\tilde\theta$ instead of at $\theta$.</Variable>
    <Variable>**$\theta, \theta'$** — the parameters before and after this step.</Variable>
    <Variable>**$\eta, \beta$** — the learning rate and momentum coefficient, same roles as in plain Momentum.</Variable>
  </Variables>

  <Steps>
    <Step label="Same bookkeeping, one different input">
      Every quantity here — $v$, $\eta$, $\beta$ — means exactly what it meant in the Momentum chapter. The
      only change is *where* the gradient is evaluated.
    </Step>
    <Step label="Step one is always identical to Momentum">
      With $v=0$ at the start, $\tilde\theta=\theta$, so the first step's gradient is evaluated at the same
      place either way — the two methods can only start splitting apart from step two onward.
    </Step>
    <Step label="Looking ahead corrects overshoot sooner">
      If the velocity is about to carry the point past the minimum, the look-ahead gradient already points
      back the other way — Nesterov reacts to that correction one step earlier than plain Momentum can.
    </Step>
  </Steps>
</Formalize>

<Play>
  <PlayDemo />

  Push β high on Momentum and watch it overshoot and oscillate. Switch to Nesterov at the same β — the
  look-ahead gradient starts pulling back sooner, which is why NAG tends to tolerate higher β before it
  starts bouncing.
</Play>

<WorkedExample>
  Starting at $(4,1)$ on $f(x,y)=x^2+10y^2$, with $\eta=0.045$, $\beta=0.7$, velocity initially zero:

  <Steps>
    <Step label="Step 1 is identical to Momentum">
      $\tilde\theta = (4,1) - 0.045(0.7)(0,0) = (4,1)$, so the gradient there is $(8,20)$, giving
      $v'=(8,20)$ and $\theta' = (4,1)-0.045(8,20) = (3.64, 0.1)$ — exactly Momentum's first step.
    </Step>
    <Step label="Step 2's look-ahead point is new">
      $\tilde\theta = (3.64,0.1) - 0.045(0.7)(8,20) = (3.64,0.1) - (0.252, 0.63) = (3.388, -0.53)$
    </Step>
    <Step label="Evaluate the gradient there, not at (3.64, 0.1)">
      $$
      \nabla f(3.388,-0.53) = (2 \times 3.388,\ 20\times(-0.53)) = (6.776,\ -10.6)
      $$
    </Step>
    <Step label="Finish the update">
      $v' = 0.7(8,20)+(6.776,-10.6) = (12.376, 3.4)$, so
      $\theta' = (3.64,0.1) - 0.045(12.376,3.4) = (3.08308, -0.053)$ — already noticeably different from
      Momentum's second step, $(3.0604, -0.62)$, purely from evaluating the gradient one point ahead.
    </Step>
  </Steps>
</WorkedExample>

<NesterovCheckpoint />

<Summary>
  $$
  \tilde\theta=\theta-\eta\beta v,\qquad v'=\beta v+\nabla f(\tilde\theta),\qquad \theta'=\theta-\eta v'
  $$

  Nesterov keeps every piece of Momentum's machinery and changes exactly one thing: where the gradient gets
  evaluated. That single change is enough to correct overshoot earlier, which is why Adam and most modern
  optimizers offer a Nesterov-style variant as a near-free upgrade over plain momentum.
</Summary>
