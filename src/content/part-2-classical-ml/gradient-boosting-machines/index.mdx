<Hook>
  AdaBoost reweights *points* — misclassified ones get louder. What if, instead of reweighting anything, each new
  model just predicted whatever the current model still gets wrong?
</Hook>

<Intuition>
  <IntuitionDemo />

  Five points, nowhere near a straight line. At 0 rounds the fit is just the flat average — bad everywhere.
  Each round after that fits a small step to whatever's still left over, and the curve bends closer to the data.
</Intuition>

<Formalize>
  This is **gradient boosting**. Start from the simplest possible model — a constant — then repeatedly fit a new
  small model to whatever the current ensemble still gets wrong, and add it in:

  $$
  F_0(x) = \bar{y}, \qquad F_m(x) = F_{m-1}(x) + h_m(x)
  $$

  <Variables>
    <Variable>**$F_0(x)$** — the starting prediction: just the training mean $\bar{y}$, before any model has run.</Variable>
    <Variable>**$F_m(x)$** — the ensemble's prediction after $m$ rounds.</Variable>
    <Variable>**$h_m(x)$** — round $m$'s new "weak learner", here a single-split regression stump.</Variable>
  </Variables>

  $h_m$ is fit to each point's **pseudo-residual** — the negative gradient of the loss with respect to the
  *current* prediction. For squared-error loss $L = \frac{1}{2}(y - F)^2$, that gradient works out to something
  completely ordinary:

  $$
  r_i^{(m)} = -\frac{\partial L}{\partial F(x_i)}\bigg|_{F = F_{m-1}} = y_i - F_{m-1}(x_i)
  $$

  <Variables>
    <Variable>**$r_i^{(m)}$** — point $i$'s pseudo-residual going into round $m$.</Variable>
    <Variable>**$y_i$** — point $i$'s true target value.</Variable>
  </Variables>

  <Steps>
    <Step label="Squared-error loss makes it literal">
      For squared-error loss, "fit the negative gradient" and "fit the ordinary leftover residual" are the exact
      same instruction — that's why the very first boosting example anyone sees is usually just "fit the
      residuals," with the gradient framing hidden underneath.
    </Step>
    <Step label="Any differentiable loss plugs into the same recipe">
      Swap in a different differentiable loss (say, one built for classification) and the pseudo-residual formula
      changes, but the outer loop — fit a small model to the current gradient, add it in, repeat — doesn't.
    </Step>
    <Step label="Every round only ever sees what's still wrong">
      Round $m$'s stump never sees $y$ directly — only $r^{(m)}$, the part of $y$ that $F_{m-1}$ hasn't explained
      yet. Whatever the ensemble already gets right stays untouched.
    </Step>
  </Steps>
</Formalize>

<Play>
  <PlayDemo />

  Watch the residual list shrink as rounds are added — this is the "pseudo-residual" input each new stump is
  actually trained on, made visible.
</Play>

<WorkedExample>
  Five points: $(1,1), (2,2), (3,8), (4,9), (5,13)$.

  <Steps>
    <Step label="F0 is just the mean">
      $\bar{y} = (1+2+8+9+13)/5 = 6.6$. Each point's residual going into round 1 is $y_i-6.6$:
      - $x=1$: $1-6.6=-5.6$
      - $x=2$: $2-6.6=-4.6$
      - $x=3$: $8-6.6=1.4$
      - $x=4$: $9-6.6=2.4$
      - $x=5$: $13-6.6=6.4$

      Squaring and summing: $(-5.6)^2+(-4.6)^2+1.4^2+2.4^2+6.4^2 = 31.36+21.16+1.96+5.76+40.96 = 101.2$.
    </Step>
    <Step label="Round 1's stump finds the split at x = 2.5">
      Splitting into $\{1,2\}$ and $\{3,4,5\}$:
      - Left group mean: $(-5.6-4.6)/2=-5.1$
      - Right group mean: $(1.4+2.4+6.4)/3=3.4$

      This beats every other candidate split by a wide margin. $F_1(x) = 6.6 - 5.1 = 1.5$ for $x < 2.5$, and
      $6.6 + 3.4 = 10$ for $x \geq 2.5$.
    </Step>
    <Step label="New residuals, and a new SSE">
      $y-F_1$ at each point:
      - $x=1$: $1-1.5=-0.5$
      - $x=2$: $2-1.5=0.5$
      - $x=3$: $8-10=-2$
      - $x=4$: $9-10=-1$
      - $x=5$: $13-10=3$

      Squaring and summing: $(-0.5)^2+0.5^2+(-2)^2+(-1)^2+3^2 = 0.25+0.25+4+1+9=14.5$ — down from $101.2$.
      Real progress, but $x=3,4,5$ are still off by as much as 3.
    </Step>
    <Step label="Round 2 fits a fresh stump to those residuals">
      Splitting at $x = 4.5$ (isolating the single worst point, $x=5$):
      - Left group mean ($x=1,2,3,4$): $(-0.5+0.5-2-1)/4=-0.75$
      - Right group mean ($x=5$): $3/1=3$

      $F_2(x) = F_1(x) - 0.75$ for $x < 4.5$, and $F_1(x) + 3 = 13$ for $x \geq 4.5$:
      - $x=1,2$: $1.5-0.75=0.75$
      - $x=3,4$: $10-0.75=9.25$
      - $x=5$: $10+3=13$

      New residuals $y-F_2$ are $0.25, 1.25, -1.25, -0.25, 0$, giving a final SSE of just
      $0.25^2+1.25^2+1.25^2+0.25^2+0^2=3.25$.
    </Step>
  </Steps>
</WorkedExample>

<GbmCheckpoint />

<Summary>
  $$
  F_m(x) = F_{m-1}(x) + h_m(x), \qquad h_m \text{ fit to } r_i^{(m)} = y_i - F_{m-1}(x_i)
  $$

  AdaBoost reweights the *data*; gradient boosting reweights nothing — it just keeps handing each new weak
  learner a smaller and smaller piece of what's left unexplained. Two rounds here cut total error from $101.2$ to
  $3.25$ — over 30x — and
  the mechanism generalizes past regression: swap the loss, and the same "fit the gradient, add it in" loop
  produces a boosted classifier instead.
</Summary>
