<Hook>
  Given a scatter of points, what's the single "best" line through them — and what does "best" even mean here?
</Hook>

<Intuition>
  <IntuitionDemo />

  Drag either end of the line. The dashed segments are each point's **residual** — how far it misses the line, straight up or down. A good fit is one where those misses are all small at once.
</Intuition>

<Formalize>
  Squaring each residual before adding them up gives the standard measure of fit — the **sum of squared errors (SSE)**:

  $$
  \text{SSE} = \sum_i \big(y_i - (w x_i + b)\big)^2
  $$

  <Variables>
    <Variable>**SSE** — the sum of squared errors: how far the line's predictions miss the actual data, added up.</Variable>
    <Variable>**$y_i$** — the actual (observed) value for data point $i$.</Variable>
    <Variable>**$w$** — the line's slope.</Variable>
    <Variable>**$x_i$** — the input value for data point $i$.</Variable>
    <Variable>**$b$** — the line's intercept.</Variable>
  </Variables>

  <Steps>
    <Step label="Every term becomes positive">
      Squaring means misses in opposite directions can't cancel out — a point the line overshoots and a point it
      undershoots both add to the total instead of offsetting each other.
    </Step>
    <Step label="One large miss costs far more than several small ones">
      Squaring also makes a big residual disproportionately expensive, so a line has to stay close to *every*
      point, not just close on average.
    </Step>
  </Steps>
</Formalize>

<Play>
  <PlayDemo />

  Drag toward a better angle and watch $w$, $b$, and the SSE update together — the line's slope and intercept are just two numbers, and SSE is one number summarizing how well those two numbers fit everything at once.
</Play>

<WorkedExample>
  With $w=2, b=1$:

  <Steps>
    <Step label="Compute the predictions">
      $\hat y_i = 2x_i+1$ at each $x$:
      - $x=1$: $2(1)+1=3$
      - $x=3$: $2(3)+1=7$
      - $x=5$: $2(5)+1=11$
      - $x=7$: $2(7)+1=15$
      - $x=9$: $2(9)+1=19$

      Against actual values $3.5, 6.8, 10.5, 15.2, 18.5$.
    </Step>
    <Step label="Compute the residuals">
      $y_i-\hat y_i$ at each point:
      - $3.5-3=0.5$
      - $6.8-7=-0.2$
      - $10.5-11=-0.5$
      - $15.2-15=0.2$
      - $18.5-19=-0.5$
    </Step>
    <Step label="Square and sum them">
      $$
      \text{SSE} = 0.25+0.04+0.25+0.04+0.25 = 0.83
      $$

      Close, clean numbers — and already a good fit, without needing the exact optimum.
    </Step>
  </Steps>
</WorkedExample>

<Summary>
  $$
  \text{SSE} = \sum_i \big(y_i - (w x_i + b)\big)^2
  $$

  Fitting a line means choosing $w$ and $b$ to make this sum as small as possible. Next: what changes if you measure "small" a different way.
</Summary>
