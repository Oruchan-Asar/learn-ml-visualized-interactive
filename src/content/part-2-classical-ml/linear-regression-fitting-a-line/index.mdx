import { Hook, Intuition, Formalize, Play, WorkedExample, Summary } from "@/components/chapter/Beats";
import { IntuitionDemo, PlayDemo, FitCheckpoint } from "./Demos";

<Hook>
  Given a scatter of points, what's the single "best" line through them — and what does "best" even mean here?
</Hook>

<Intuition>
  <IntuitionDemo />

  Drag either end of the line. The dashed segments are each point's **residual** — how far it misses the line, straight up or down. A good fit is one where those misses are all small at once.
</Intuition>

<Formalize>
  Squaring each residual before adding them up gives the standard measure of fit —

  $$
  \text{SSE} = \sum_i \big(y_i - (w x_i + b)\big)^2
  $$

  Squaring does two things: it makes every term positive, so misses in opposite directions can't cancel out, and it makes one large miss cost far more than several small ones — a line has to stay close to *every* point, not just close on average.
</Formalize>

<Play>
  <PlayDemo />

  Drag toward a better angle and watch $w$, $b$, and the SSE update together — the line's slope and intercept are just two numbers, and SSE is one number summarizing how well those two numbers fit everything at once.
</Play>

<WorkedExample>
  With $w=2, b=1$: predictions are $3, 7, 11, 15, 19$ against actual values $3.5, 6.8, 10.5, 15.2, 18.5$. The residuals are $0.5, -0.2, -0.5, 0.2, -0.5$, and squaring and summing them gives

  $$
  \text{SSE} = 0.25+0.04+0.25+0.04+0.25 = 0.83
  $$

  Close, clean numbers — and already a good fit, without needing the exact optimum.
</WorkedExample>
