<Hook>
  A tree that gets 100% of its training data right sounds perfect. Is it?
</Hook>

<Intuition>
  <IntuitionDemo />

  The top row is training data — five of those points are quietly mislabeled, noise a real dataset would have. Drag depth up: watch the tree stop finding *the* boundary and start carving out tiny one-point regions instead, each one built to rescue a single mislabeled point.
</Intuition>

<Formalize>
  Nothing stops a tree from growing until every training point is classified perfectly — split enough times and you can isolate any single point into its own leaf. The question isn't whether it *can* memorize noise, it's whether doing so helps on data it hasn't seen:

  $$
  \text{gap} = \text{accuracy}_{\text{train}} - \text{accuracy}_{\text{validation}}
  $$

  A widening gap as depth grows is the signature of memorizing the training set's specific noise rather than the pattern underneath it.
</Formalize>

<Play>
  <PlayDemo />

  The bottom row is validation data the tree never trained on — clean, no mislabeled points. Depth 1 or 2 already gets every single one right. Push past that and watch validation accuracy start dropping *while training accuracy keeps climbing* — the tree is spending its extra depth on the five noisy points, and each region it carves out to rescue one of them swallows nearby validation points too.
</Play>

<WorkedExample>
  At depth 1, the tree finds one split at $x = 9.5$ — exactly the true boundary. That single split gets $20/25 = 80\%$ of training points right (the 5 mislabeled ones are the only misses) and $24/24 = 100\%$ of validation points right, since the boundary itself is exactly correct.

  At depth 6, the tree has carved out enough regions to rescue 4 of those 5 noisy points, reaching $24/25 = 96\%$ training accuracy — an improvement! But validation accuracy has *fallen* to $20/24 \approx 83\%$: the tiny regions built around noise now misclassify real validation points that happen to fall inside them.
</WorkedExample>

<Summary>
  $$
  \text{gap} = \text{accuracy}_{\text{train}} - \text{accuracy}_{\text{validation}}
  $$

  Training accuracy alone can't tell memorization apart from generalization — a tree can always reach 100% on data it's already seen. Validation accuracy, measured on held-out data, is what catches it: it improves while the tree is learning the real pattern, then reverses once the tree starts learning the noise instead.
</Summary>
