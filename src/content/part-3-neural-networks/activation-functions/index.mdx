<Hook>
  The perceptron's sign function gives a hard yes/no. What if a neuron needs to answer "mostly yes," or needs a slope to learn from at all?
</Hook>

<Intuition>
  <IntuitionDemo />

  Drag the input across all four at once. Step jumps instantly between 0 and 1 — no in-between. Sigmoid and tanh curve smoothly through the middle. **ReLU** — short for **Rectified Linear Unit** — does something different again: it's flat at exactly 0 for every negative input, then rises like a plain diagonal line.
</Intuition>

<Formalize>
  An activation function turns a neuron's raw weighted sum into its output. Four of the most common:

  $$
  \text{step}(x) = \begin{cases} 0 & x<0 \\ 1 & x\ge 0 \end{cases}, \quad
  \sigma(x) = \frac{1}{1+e^{-x}}, \quad
  \tanh(x), \quad
  \text{ReLU}(x) = \max(0,x)
  $$

  <Variables>
    <Variable>**$x$** — the neuron's raw weighted sum, before activation.</Variable>
    <Variable>**$\text{step}(x)$** — the perceptron's hard yes/no rule: 0 below zero, 1 at or above it.</Variable>
    <Variable>**$\sigma(x)$** — the sigmoid, a smooth curve ranging over $(0,1)$.</Variable>
    <Variable>**$\tanh(x)$** — a smooth curve ranging over $(-1,1)$.</Variable>
    <Variable>**$\text{ReLU}(x)$** — zero for negative inputs, and equal to $x$ itself for positive ones.</Variable>
  </Variables>

  Step is what the perceptron used — but it's flat everywhere except one point, so its slope is useless for learning by gradient. Sigmoid and tanh are smooth substitutes with a real slope everywhere. ReLU throws out smoothness for something else: dead simple to compute, and its slope never shrinks to nothing on the positive side.
</Formalize>

<Play>
  <PlayDemo />

  Watch what happens right around $x=0$. Step is discontinuous there. Sigmoid and tanh are at their steepest — right where they're most useful for learning. ReLU has a kink: flat on the left, a clean 45° line on the right.
</Play>

<WorkedExample>
  Tanh isn't a fourth independent shape — it's sigmoid, rescaled: $\tanh(x) = 2\sigma(2x)-1$.

  <Steps>
    <Step label="Evaluate sigmoid at 2x">
      At $x=1$: $\sigma(2) = 1/(1+e^{-2}) \approx 0.881$.
    </Step>
    <Step label="Rescale it into tanh's range">
      $2(0.881)-1 = 0.762$ — exactly $\tanh(1)$. Sigmoid ranges over $(0,1)$; tanh stretches and shifts that same
      curve to range over $(-1,1)$.
    </Step>
  </Steps>
</WorkedExample>

<Summary>
  $$
  \sigma(x) = \frac{1}{1+e^{-x}}, \qquad \text{ReLU}(x) = \max(0,x)
  $$

  Every neuron in every network from here on is a weighted sum fed through one of these. The choice matters enormously once networks get deep — the next chapters are about exactly what goes wrong (or right) when you stack many of these on top of each other.
</Summary>
