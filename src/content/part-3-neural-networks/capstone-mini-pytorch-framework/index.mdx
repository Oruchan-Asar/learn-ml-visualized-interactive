<Formalize>
  **AdamW** is Adam plus one change: weight decay applied directly to the parameter, decoupled from the
  gradient-based moment estimates.

  $$
  \hat m = \frac{m}{1-\beta_1^t}, \qquad \hat v = \frac{v}{1-\beta_2^t}, \qquad \theta \leftarrow \theta - \eta\left(\frac{\hat m}{\sqrt{\hat v}+\epsilon} + \lambda\theta\right)
  $$

  <Variables>
    <Variable>**$\hat m, \hat v$** — Chapter 9's bias-corrected running averages of the gradient and its square.</Variable>
    <Variable>**$\theta$** — the parameter being updated: any single weight or bias in the network's Tensor.</Variable>
    <Variable>**$\eta$** — the learning rate.</Variable>
    <Variable>**$\lambda$** — the weight decay coefficient, shrinking every parameter a little on every step.</Variable>
  </Variables>

  <Steps>
    <Step label="Everything except one term is Adam, unchanged">
      $\hat m/(\sqrt{\hat v}+\epsilon)$ is exactly Chapter 9's Adam update — nothing new there.
    </Step>
    <Step label="The new piece: decoupled weight decay">
      $\lambda\theta$ is added straight onto the update, applied to the raw parameter — not folded into the
      gradient the way plain L2 regularization would be. That decoupling is the "W" in AdamW.
    </Step>
    <Step label="One update rule, every parameter in the network">
      The same formula runs once per weight and bias — 17 parameters total in this tiny network, billions in a
      real one — each keeping its own $m$, $v$, and $t$, all driven by the one gradient backprop computed for it.
    </Step>
  </Steps>
</Formalize>

<WorkedExample>
  From this network's fixed random start, before any training:

  <Steps>
    <Step label="Pick one parameter: the weight from hidden neuron 1 to the output">
      $\theta = 0.166168$. A full backward pass through all four XOR points gives its gradient:
      $g = -0.012081$.
    </Step>
    <Step label="At the very first step, the moving averages start from zero">
      $t=1$, so $\hat m = m/(1-\beta_1) = g$ exactly, and $\hat v = v/(1-\beta_2) = g^2$ exactly — the bias
      correction perfectly cancels the very first $(1-\beta)$ factor on both averages.
    </Step>
    <Step label="That collapses the ratio to just the gradient's sign">
      $\hat m/\sqrt{\hat v} = g/|g| = -1$ — same "first step ignores magnitude" behavior Chapter 9 found for
      RMSProp and Adam.
    </Step>
    <Step label="Add the weight decay term and take the step">
      With $\eta=0.1$ and $\lambda=0.01$: update $= 0.1\,(-1 + 0.01\times0.166168) \approx -0.099834$, so
      $\theta \leftarrow 0.166168 - (-0.099834) = 0.266002$.
    </Step>
  </Steps>
</WorkedExample>

<Summary>
  $$
  \theta \leftarrow \theta - \eta\left(\frac{\hat m}{\sqrt{\hat v}+\epsilon} + \lambda\theta\right)
  $$

  Forward pass, loss, backprop, and an optimizer step, run in a loop until the loss is small enough — that's
  every framework's training loop, from this four-line XOR network to a model with billions of parameters.
  Swapping AdamW in for plain gradient descent didn't change any of the pieces this part built by hand; it
  only changed how the last piece, the update rule, turns a gradient into a step. Part IV starts asking what
  happens when the *pieces themselves* get more specialized, starting with images.
</Summary>
