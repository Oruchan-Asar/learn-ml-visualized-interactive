<Hook>
  LayerNorm computes a mean and a variance every single forward pass. What if the mean-centering step was never
  buying you much in the first place?
</Hook>

<Intuition>
  <IntuitionDemo />

  Same six activations as the last chapter — mean $0.5$, not $0$. LayerNorm would force the output's mean back to
  $0$. RMSNorm doesn't: it never subtracts a mean at all, so whatever bias was in the input mean survives into the
  output, just rescaled.
</Intuition>

<Formalize>
  RMSNorm divides by the root-mean-square of the vector — no mean subtraction anywhere in the formula:

  $$
  \hat h_i = \frac{h_i}{\text{RMS}(h)} \cdot g, \qquad \text{RMS}(h) = \sqrt{\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^n h_j^2 + \epsilon}
  $$

  <Variables>
    <Variable>**$h_i$** — channel $i$'s raw value.</Variable>
    <Variable>**RMS($h$)** — the root-mean-square of the *whole* vector: the square root of its average squared
    value.</Variable>
    <Variable>**$g$** — a learned (here, shared-scalar) gain applied after normalizing.</Variable>
    <Variable>**$\epsilon$** — a tiny constant keeping the division well-defined near zero.</Variable>
  </Variables>

  <Steps>
    <Step label="LayerNorm: subtract the mean, then divide by the standard deviation">
      $\hat h_i = (h_i-\mu)/\sigma$ — two statistics computed from the data, one subtraction, one division.
    </Step>
    <Step label="RMSNorm: skip the subtraction entirely">
      $\hat h_i = h_i/\text{RMS}(h)$ — one statistic, one division. Whatever mean the input had is still there,
      just divided down along with everything else.
    </Step>
  </Steps>
</Formalize>

<Play>
  <PlayDemo />

  Drag the gain. Every one of the six channels scales up or down by exactly the same factor — RMSNorm never treats
  any channel differently based on where the group's center happens to sit, because it never computed a center.
</Play>

<WorkedExample>
  <Steps>
    <Step label="RMS of the six activations">
      $[2,-1,0.5,3,-2.5,1]$ has mean-of-squares $21.5/6\approx3.583$, so $\text{RMS}\approx1.893$.
    </Step>
    <Step label="RMSNorm's output mean isn't 0">
      Dividing every value by $1.893$ divides the *mean* by $1.893$ too: $0.5/1.893\approx0.264$. LayerNorm on the
      identical input forces that same quantity to exactly $0$ by construction.
    </Step>
    <Step label="One fewer statistic, one fewer reduction">
      RMSNorm needs only $\sum h_j^2$, not both $\sum h_j$ and $\sum(h_j-\mu)^2$ — cheaper to compute, and in
      practice competitive with LayerNorm's accuracy on large transformer models, which is why models like LLaMA
      and T5 use it instead.
    </Step>
  </Steps>
</WorkedExample>

<Summary>
  $$
  \hat h_i = \frac{h_i}{\text{RMS}(h)} \cdot g
  $$

  RMSNorm keeps normalization's core benefit — controlling the *scale* of activations layer after layer — while
  dropping the assumption that re-centering to mean zero was doing meaningful work. One fewer statistic to compute,
  one fewer place for a bug to hide, and empirically little accuracy lost. The next few chapters turn from
  activation stability to a different training concern: keeping the model from simply memorizing its training data.
</Summary>
