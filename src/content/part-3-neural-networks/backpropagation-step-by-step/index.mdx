<Hook>
  Last chapter chained two neurons along one path. A real network has *many* paths — every weight needs its own gradient. Is that a completely new problem, or the same one, more times?
</Hook>

<Intuition>
  <IntuitionDemo />

  This is the loss of a real two-layer network — 2 inputs, 2 hidden neurons, 1 output — as a function of just one weight, $w_{11}$, holding everything else fixed. Drag it: the tangent line is backprop's answer for $dL/dw_{11}$, computed by chaining through *both* layers.
</Intuition>

<Formalize>
  Backpropagation is the chain rule, applied backward from the loss, one layer at a time:

  $$
  \frac{\partial L}{\partial w_{11}} = \frac{\partial L}{\partial y}\cdot\frac{\partial y}{\partial z_{\text{out}}}\cdot\frac{\partial z_{\text{out}}}{\partial h_1}\cdot\frac{\partial h_1}{\partial z_1}\cdot\frac{\partial z_1}{\partial w_{11}}
  $$

  <Variables>
    <Variable>**$L$** — the loss, measuring how wrong the network's prediction is.</Variable>
    <Variable>**$w_{11}$** — the weight connecting input 1 to hidden neuron 1, the one being differentiated.</Variable>
    <Variable>**$y$** — the network's final output.</Variable>
    <Variable>**$z_{\text{out}}$** — the output neuron's raw weighted sum, before its activation.</Variable>
    <Variable>**$h_1$** — hidden neuron 1's activation, feeding into the output.</Variable>
    <Variable>**$z_1$** — hidden neuron 1's raw weighted sum, before its activation.</Variable>
  </Variables>

  <Steps>
    <Step label="The loss adds a new starting term">
      Five factors total, but only two are genuinely *new* beyond Chapter 4: the loss contributes its own local
      derivative, $\partial L/\partial y$, at the very start of the chain.
    </Step>
    <Step label="One factor is just a connecting weight">
      $\partial z_{\text{out}}/\partial h_1$ is nothing more than $v_1$ — the weight connecting $h_1$ to the
      output. Everything else in the five-factor product is the same local-slope idea from Chapter 4.
    </Step>
    <Step label="Every weight reuses the same shared terms">
      Every other weight in the network gets its gradient the same way, reusing the exact same
      $\partial L/\partial z_{\text{out}}$ and $\partial L/\partial z_1$ terms computed once and shared.
    </Step>
  </Steps>
</Formalize>

<Play>
  <PlayDemo />

  Push $w_{11}$ negative and watch the loss keep falling — this particular network, on this particular input, never stops preferring a more negative $w_{11}$ within this range. The gradient still tells you the *direction* correctly at every point, even where it isn't near zero.
</Play>

<WorkedExample>
  At $w_{11}=0.5$:

  <Steps>
    <Step label="Forward pass">
      $z_1 = 0.5(1) + (-0.3)(2) + 0.1 = 0$ exactly, so $h_1=\sigma(0)=0.5$. Forward through the rest:
      - $z_2 = -0.2(1)+0.4(2)+0.2 = 0.8$, so $h_2=\sigma(0.8)\approx0.690$
      - $z_{\text{out}} = 1.5(0.5)+(-1.0)(0.690)+0.3 \approx 0.360$
      - $y=\sigma(0.360)\approx0.589$
      - loss $=0.5(y-0)^2 \approx 0.173$
    </Step>
    <Step label="Backward into the output neuron">
      $\partial L/\partial y = y = 0.589$ (target is $0$), times $\sigma'(z_{\text{out}})=y(1-y)\approx0.242$
      gives $\partial L/\partial z_{\text{out}}\approx0.143$. That one number is *shared* by every weight
      feeding the output:
      - $\partial L/\partial v_1 = 0.143\cdot h_1 \approx 0.071$
      - $\partial L/\partial v_2 = 0.143\cdot h_2\approx0.098$
      - $\partial L/\partial c \approx0.143$
    </Step>
    <Step label="Push further back, into the hidden neurons">
      For hidden neuron 1: $\partial L/\partial h_1 = 0.143\cdot v_1\approx0.214$, times
      $\sigma'(z_1)=h_1(1-h_1)=0.25$ gives $\partial L/\partial z_1\approx0.053$ — shared by every weight
      feeding $h_1$:
      - $\partial L/\partial w_{11}\approx0.053(1)=0.053$
      - $\partial L/\partial w_{12}\approx0.053(2)=0.107$
      - $\partial L/\partial b_1\approx0.053$

      The exact same mechanism, run on neuron 2: $\partial L/\partial h_2 = 0.143\cdot v_2\approx-0.143$,
      times $\sigma'(z_2)=h_2(1-h_2)\approx0.214$ gives $\partial L/\partial z_2\approx-0.030$ — shared by
      every weight feeding $h_2$:
      - $\partial L/\partial w_{21}\approx-0.030(1)=-0.030$
      - $\partial L/\partial w_{22}\approx-0.030(2)=-0.061$
      - $\partial L/\partial b_2\approx-0.030$
    </Step>
    <Step label="Tally the result">
      All nine gradients, from two shared numbers ($\partial L/\partial z_{\text{out}}$, and per hidden
      neuron $\partial L/\partial z_1$ or $\partial L/\partial z_2$) and the inputs each weight multiplies.
    </Step>
  </Steps>
</WorkedExample>

<BackpropCheckpoint />

<Summary>
  $$
  \frac{\partial L}{\partial w_{11}} = \frac{\partial L}{\partial y}\cdot\frac{\partial y}{\partial z_{\text{out}}}\cdot\frac{\partial z_{\text{out}}}{\partial h_1}\cdot\frac{\partial h_1}{\partial z_1}\cdot\frac{\partial z_1}{\partial w_{11}}
  $$

  Every gradient in this chapter was checked against a plain numerical derivative — nudge a weight by a tiny amount, remeasure the loss, compare. That's the whole promise of **autograd** (short for **automatic differentiation**): whatever backprop computes analytically, a numerical check should reproduce. The next chapters ask what starts to go wrong with this chain once a network gets deep enough that it's dozens of layers long, not two.
</Summary>
