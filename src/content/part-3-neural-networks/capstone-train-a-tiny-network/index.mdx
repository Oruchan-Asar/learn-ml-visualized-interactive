<Hook>
  Chapter 1 of this part built a perceptron — a single neuron drawing one straight line. Here are four points a single straight line can never separate. Can a whole network do what one neuron can't?
</Hook>

<Intuition>
  <IntuitionDemo />

  This is **XOR** — short for **exclusive OR**: $(0,0)$ and $(1,1)$ are one class, $(0,1)$ and $(1,0)$ are the other — diagonal pairs, impossible for any line to separate. Click train and watch the shaded region fold itself around the two "on" corners, something a single neuron's straight boundary could never do.
</Intuition>

<Formalize>
  The network here is tiny — 2 inputs, 4 hidden neurons, 1 output, all sigmoid — but the training loop is exactly
  the same loop that trains networks a billion times its size:

  $$
  \text{forward} \to \text{loss} \to \text{backward} \to \text{update} \to \text{repeat}
  $$

  <Steps>
    <Step label="Forward pass through the hidden layer">
      Chapter 3's forward pass, computing every neuron's activation from the input.
    </Step>
    <Step label="Backprop computes every gradient">
      Chapter 5's backpropagation, computing every weight's gradient at once.
    </Step>
    <Step label="Gradient descent applies the update">
      Part I's gradient descent, applying the update backprop just computed.
    </Step>
    <Step label="Repeat">
      Every piece here is one you've already built in this part — the loop just keeps running, and it's the exact
      same loop that trains networks a billion times this size.
    </Step>
  </Steps>
</Formalize>

<Play>
  <PlayDemo />

  Watch the four individual outputs, not just the picture. Two need to climb toward $1$, two need to fall toward $0$ — and they all move together, epoch after epoch, driven by the exact same backward pass computing all of their gradients at once.
</Play>

<WorkedExample>
  <Steps>
    <Step label="Watch the loss over training">
      From this network's particular random start, a single epoch barely moves the loss. By epoch 400 it's down to
      about $0.087$; by epoch 800, about $0.006$ — and every one of the four points is already on the correct side
      of $0.5$.
    </Step>
    <Step label="See what makes it possible">
      The hidden layer is what makes this possible at all: each of its four neurons draws its *own* straight line
      through the input space, and the output layer combines those four lines into a boundary no single line could
      ever be.
    </Step>
  </Steps>
</WorkedExample>
