<Formalize>
  "Good enough" needs a metric. Chamfer distance scores how well a predicted point set $A$ matches a target
  $B$, symmetrically — it penalizes both a predicted point sitting far from *any* real surface point, and a
  real surface point that nothing predicted is near:

  $$
  \text{chamfer}(A, B) = \frac{1}{|A|}\sum_{a \in A} \min_{b \in B} \|a-b\| \;+\; \frac{1}{|B|}\sum_{b \in B} \min_{a \in A} \|b-a\|
  $$

  Once a shape is refined enough, turning it (or a continuous density field) into an actual mesh means
  finding where the field crosses zero — the surface. On a 1D slice, that's linear interpolation between
  two samples of opposite sign:

  $$
  x_{\text{surface}} = x_0 + \frac{-d_0}{d_1 - d_0}(x_1 - x_0)
  $$

  <Variables>
    <Variable>**$A, B$** — the predicted point set and the target (or vice versa); the two terms make the metric symmetric.</Variable>
    <Variable>**$d_0, d_1$** — a sampled field's value at two adjacent points $x_0, x_1$ (negative = outside, positive = inside).</Variable>
    <Variable>**$x_{\text{surface}}$** — where the field crosses zero between those two samples — marching cubes' core 1D building block.</Variable>
  </Variables>

  <Steps>
    <Step label="Coarse guess: all 4 points collapsed onto the centroid (1,1)">
      Every point is $\sqrt{2}$ from every one of the target square's 4 corners in both directions:
      chamfer $= \sqrt{2} + \sqrt{2} = 2\sqrt{2} \approx 2.828$.
    </Step>
    <Step label="Halfway refined">
      Each point has moved halfway to its corner: chamfer drops to exactly $\sqrt{2} \approx 1.414$ — half
      the coarse score.
    </Step>
    <Step label="Fully refined: points exactly on the corners">
      chamfer $= 0$ — every predicted point sits exactly on a real surface point, and vice versa.
    </Step>
  </Steps>
</Formalize>

<Play>
  <PlayDemo />

  Drag $t$ continuously instead of jumping between 3 fixed steps. Chamfer distance falls off perfectly
  linearly here — $2(1-t)\sqrt{2}$ — because every point is moving in a straight line toward its own
  target corner at the same rate.
</Play>

<WorkedExample>
  Locating a surface crossing in a 1D density profile sampled at $x = 0, 1, 2, 3$ with values $-1, -0.2,
  0.6, 1.5$ (negative = outside, positive = inside):

  <Steps>
    <Step label="Between x=1 (d=-0.2) and x=2 (d=0.6): opposite signs, a real crossing">
      $x_{\text{surface}} = 1 + \frac{0.2}{0.6-(-0.2)}(2-1) = 1 + 0.25 = 1.25$ — the surface passes a
      quarter of the way from $x=1$ to $x=2$.
    </Step>
    <Step label="Between x=0 (d=-1) and x=1 (d=-0.2): same sign, no real crossing">
      The formula still returns a number ($1.25$ again, by coincidence of these particular values), but
      it falls *outside* the segment $[0, 1]$ — the tell that there's nothing to mesh here, since both
      samples agree they're outside the shape.
    </Step>
  </Steps>
</WorkedExample>
